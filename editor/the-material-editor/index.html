The material editor

A material is often made before the scene that will show it, or for several of them. The material editor makes materials alone: no level, no viewport, the material is the document. It is a profile of the shell (material-editor, see Profiles and views) on the editor of material graphs of Editing a material: the same canvas, the same nodes, the same code.

Here it is with a brushed copper open, previewed on the gearbox of Measuring and cutting under the HDR panorama of a photo studio. Nothing is kept when the page is left.

Open the editor in a page of its own

1. The profile

Section titled “1. The profile”

The profile loads three plugins: the workbench, the material graphs (fdw.materialGraph) and the console. There is no scene: the engine starts without a viewport and draws only the previews, in textures read back. The layout: the materials in the middle, their preview at the right, the console below; the menus File, Edit, Preview and View.

2. Materials in tabs

Section titled “2. Materials in tabs”

Each material is a document in a tab, with its own history: undoing in one never undoes in another.

Command Key What it does
File → New material Ctrl+N A new graph, a PBR surface and its color
File → Open a material… Ctrl+O A .material.json; the tab of the same file if open
File → Save the material Ctrl+S The graph as JSON; the version before for the compare
File → Close the material Ctrl+W Asks first when it has changes not saved
Edit → Undo, Edit → Redo Those of the material shown (Ctrl+Z in its canvas)

A tab shows • while its material is changed; the status bar says its name, whether it is modified, and whether it is valid or has errors. The New, Open and Save buttons of the bar of the canvas open tabs too.

Try it: make a new material, add a node (Space over the canvas), then go back to the copper: its history is untouched.

3. The preview

Section titled “3. The preview”

The Material preview panel draws the material shown, as its settings say. Drag the image to turn around the object, the wheel to come closer.

  • Object: a sphere, a cube, a plane, or a model of yours (Model…, or Preview → Preview on a model…): read by the conversion, the formats the viewer opens (glTF today), its visible meshes centered and scaled to the size of the sphere. A material is judged on the real shape: a material that reads the uv of the meshes is refused on a model without them, and says so.
  • Environment: none (the light and the ambient alone), a studio or a sky made by the engine, or an HDR panorama of yours (HDR…, a Radiance .hdr file, equirectangular). It lights the materials as in the scenes (Environment of the engine, prefiltered on the GPU); its intensity multiplies it.
  • Background: a plain color, or the environment itself.
  • Light, Light from, Light height, Ambient: the directional light and the flat ambient.
  • Size: the side of the image, from 128 to 1024 pixels; Animate plays the time of the engine (the lava of the gallery flows).

The settings are not part of the material: changing them never changes it. They are the same for every material, and kept with the layout. Preview → Save the preview settings… writes them to a file, the same bytes for the same settings; Open preview settings… reads them back; Reset the preview returns to the defaults:

A file names its model and its panorama without their bytes: opened in another session, the preview asks to open them again.

4. Comparing

Section titled “4. Comparing”

Preview → Compare with the saved version puts beside the material its version as opened or last saved, in a session of its own; the list Compare of the panel chooses another material open instead. The two images take the same settings: they turn together. Change the tint of the copper, then compare: the saved version keeps the old color.

5. Importing and exporting

Section titled “5. Importing and exporting”

The materials go in and out by the conversion (The pivot model), never by readers of their own:

  • File → Import materials… reads a file of another format (glTF), and opens each of its materials, and each instance with the values of its parent, in a tab. Their values become the defaults of the uniforms of their graph. A texture has no place in a material of the editor yet: each one is a line of the report.
  • File → Export as glTF… writes the material shown in a .glb, on a sphere (the tools show only what a mesh uses). A graph that is the PBR of glTF is written as such; any other is degraded: kept whole in the FDW_scene extension (read back by FdwEngine), approached for the other tools, and said.

Each conversion writes its report in the console: what was lost, degraded or given a default, then a summary. Save keeps the graph as it is, without loss.
