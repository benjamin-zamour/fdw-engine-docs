Saving a render

A final render gives an image in linear colors, with more than its colors: what covers each pixel (its alpha), and what the denoiser was guided by. Save the render… writes them as files: in a format chosen by usage, with the passes for the retouching, a density for print and a record of what made the render, in a folder.

Here is the roller unit staged for a catalog (roller-catalog.staging.json): a transparent background and a shadow catcher under it. Its camera Hero is rendered in 480×270, 32 samples, once the model is loaded; the Render panel at the bottom shows the image and its outputs.

Open the editor in a page of its own

1. The alpha: the product and its shadow

Section titled “1. The alpha: the product and its shadow”

The path tracer tells, for each pixel, how much of it the scene covers:

  • a surface of the product covers it;
  • where the camera sees nothing, a transparent background leaves it empty (a background color or the environment covers it);
  • a shadow catcher is not seen: it adds only its shadow, as opaque as the light of the lights it does not receive, times its Shadow opacity; what is behind is seen through the rest.

The image shown in the panel has this alpha: the product and its soft shadow over the page, ready to be laid on any background. The preview traces it too: the checkerboard of the views shows through.

2. The usage

Section titled “2. The usage”

Usage sets the format and its options at once:

Usage Format For
Web JPEG, quality 85, 72 dpi Light pages; no alpha: laid on white, said
Catalog PNG 8 bits, 300 dpi The alpha of a transparent background, the layout of a page
Print PNG 16 bits, 300 dpi Smooth gradients on paper
Retouching OpenEXR half, all the passes Linear values above 1, its passes as layers of one file

Change a setting and the usage becomes Custom. The settings are kept with the layout of the editor.

3. The formats

Section titled “3. The formats”
  • PNG: 8 or 16 bits per channel, tone mapped as the views; the alpha kept when the image has one.
  • JPEG: a quality from 1 to 100. JPEG has no alpha: a transparent background is laid on white, and the panel says it.
  • OpenEXR: the linear values, never tone mapped (an image brighter than 1 stays so for the retouching), in half or float floats, its colors multiplied by the alpha as the format wants.

Density is written in the file (the pHYs of PNG, the JFIF header of JPEG, the xDensity of OpenEXR): a print layout places the image at its size, 2480 × 3508 pixels at 300 dpi for an A4 page.

4. The passes

Section titled “4. The passes”

Check the Passes to save besides the color:

Pass In a PNG or a JPEG In the OpenEXR file
Albedo The base color of the surfaces seen, no lighting albedo.R, albedo.G, albedo.B
Normal Their normal in the world, −1…1 as 0…1 (green up) N.X, N.Y, N.Z
Depth Their distance, white the nearest, black the farthest Z, in scene units
Objects A color per object, to select it in one click object.id, its identifier
Mask The alpha, in gray mask.A

In PNG and JPEG, each pass is a file of its own (Roller catalog-Hero-depth.png); where nothing is hit, the pixels of the albedo, the normal and the depth are transparent. The range of the depth (its white and its black, in scene units) is in its metadata. In OpenEXR, they are layers of the same file, read by the compositing tools.

5. The names and the folder

Section titled “5. The names and the folder”

File name is the name of the files, without their extension: {staging}, {camera}, {width}, {height}, {date}, {time} and {pass} are replaced ({staging}-{camera} by default); a pass is added at the end when the name has no {pass}. A name already in the folder is never overwritten: the files of the render become … (2), together.

The first save asks for the folder (Choose…, or View → Choose the render folder…); the next saves go there until the editor is closed. Where the browser gives no folder (no File System Access API), each file is saved where you choose. The panel says what was written, and what an encoder could not keep. Several cameras, or a turntable, are written there one after the other by the queue.

6. What made the render

Section titled “6. What made the render”

Each file says where it comes from, in its metadata (the text of a PNG, the comments of a JPEG, the attributes of an OpenEXR): the staging and its model, the camera, its pose, the criteria, the samples, the denoiser, the date. With A record beside, a .render.json file keeps the whole staging as it was (its cameras, its lighting, its materials), the settings of the render and of its files, and the names of the files: what it takes to make the render again.
