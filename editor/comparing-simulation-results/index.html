Comparing simulation results

A solver writes some fields; the question is often about another one (the largest principal stress, how close the steel is to yielding), or about the change from a case to another. This tutorial follows Showing simulation results with the same simulation profile, on the steel cantilever of 1 m under three load cases of 20 kN (models/cantilever/cantilever-cases.vtu.series): at the tip downward, spread along the beam, at the tip sideways. The Derived fields panel is at the left, under Results; Compare at the right, under the other panels.

Open the cases, then derive and compare:

  1. In Results, Steps are load cases: the three files of the series are cases, not times. The stress is shown, by von Mises, case 1.
  2. In Derived fields, New Tensor number, Of stress, Number max principal, then Add the field: stress max principal is in the list, with its formula (σ1 of stress). Choose it in Results: tension at the top of the root, almost none below.
  3. New Formula, Formula stress.vonMises / 250e6, Name utilization, then Add the field: the stress over the yield strength of the steel (250 MPa), without a unit. Show it: 0.3 at the root.
  4. Show the stress again; in Compare, With step 2, then check Side by side: a second view opens, case 2 in it, case 1 in the first, both in the colors of one legend, their cameras linked.
  5. Uncheck Side by side, then Show the difference: stress − step 2, the stress of case 1 minus that of case 2, entry by entry.

1. Derived fields

Section titled “1. Derived fields”

A derived field is computed from the fields read, at each step shown, by the engine; it is never stored (it would drift from its source) and always written with its formula in Derived fields (and marked derived in Results). It is then a field like the others: in contours, in the legend, probed, cut, its curve drawn. Four kinds:

  • Tensor number: a number of a tensor at each entry: the max, mid and min principal values (the eigenvalues of its symmetric part, the largest first), Tresca (σ1 − σ3), the pressure (−(xx + yy + zz) / 3). In the unit of the tensor, where it is (by cell here).
  • Formula: an expression of the fields, at each entry: numbers, `+ -
    • / ^, parentheses, functions (sqrt, abs, min, max, log, exp, sin…) and pi. A field by its name (in quotes when it is not a word: “part a/stress”), a number of it by [i]or a name:displacement[2], displacement.z, stress.xx, stress.vonMises, velocity.magnitude`. Its unit is the one you give, or none: never guessed.
  • Gradient: of a field at the points (or a number of it), in each 3D cell, by the linear interpolation of its tetrahedra: a vector by cell, per unit of length of the mesh.
  • Difference: a field at the step shown minus a field at a step.

What cannot be computed is refused, with the reason, in red in the panel: a field the results do not have, a formula that does not read (where it stops), fields by cell and at the points together (average one of them first), a gradient of values by cell, another unit (MPa against Pa: never converted). A derived field may name those defined before it; each is kept with the layout, and removed by its ✕.

An entry without a value (a failed cell, a step a field lacks) stays without a value in what is derived from it: never filled in.

2. Side by side

Section titled “2. Side by side”

Compare chooses what the field shown is compared with: another step (With step), and the same field or another one (Of). Side by side opens the second view of the viewport: the other side there, the field shown in the first one, in the same colors (the range of the legend fitted to both sides, and the notes say so), each legend writing its side. The cameras are linked: turn one, the other follows. With a deformation, each side is deformed by its own displacement, at the same factor. The second view shows the contours only: the cut, the iso-surfaces, the glyphs and the streamlines stay in the first.

3. The difference

Section titled “3. The difference”

Show the difference makes a derived field of the difference (the field at the step shown minus the field compared at its step) and shows it: its legend goes through zero, where the cases agree. Moving the step shown moves the first term only. The difference is computed only where the meshes correspond: one mesh, or two meshes with the same points (within a millionth of their size) and the same cells, matched by the numbers of the solver when the file has them. Otherwise it is refused, and Compare says why.

The study of the mesh below is one collection of two parts: the beam of the cases, and a coarser one (10 × 2 × 2 cells) beside it, under the first case. Show the stress of the coarse beam (the second stress of Results), then in Derived fields, New Difference, Of that stress, Minus stress (fine/stress): refused, the supports differ: 99 points against 336. A difference across two meshes would be an interpolation, a value made up; compare them side by side instead, each on its own cells.

4. In a host

Section titled “4. In a host”

The derived fields and the comparison are those of the plugin fdw.simulation and of its part in the engine, simulationExtension() (see Showing simulation results); View has Compare side by side or not. A host computes the same without the panels: DerivedFields over a FieldCache (the derived fields of a set, their values at a step), parseFormula, principalValues, cellGradients; matchSupports of @fdw/pivot-results tells whether two result meshes correspond, and why not. The side by side is drawn by the engine in the view viewport.2: an extension of the engine leaves layers out of a view (hiddenLayers) and draws other items in it (items(view)).

The files of this tutorial are written by conversion/pivot-vtk/scripts/make-cantilever.mjs: the beam theory, not a simulation.
