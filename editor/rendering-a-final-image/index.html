Rendering a final image

The preview traces the view, at the size of the window. A final render traces the camera of the staging at the size of its output (4K UHD, A4 at 300 dpi…), in a task of its own: the image is cut into tiles rendered one after the other, each gathering samples until a criterion is reached, then the whole image is denoised.

Here is the roller unit of the preview (roller-render.staging.json), its camera Hero given a small output (480×270) so that this page renders it in a moment, even on a GPU in software: the render starts once the model is loaded, until 32 samples, in the Render panel at the bottom.

Open the editor in a page of its own

1. Render the camera

Section titled “1. Render the camera”

Look through a camera of the staging (the Cameras panel), then Render the camera: the button of the Render panel, the toolbar, or View → Render the camera. The camera is rendered as it is in the staging, at its output size, whatever the size of the window; the views are rasterized while it runs, the GPU left to the render.

What is rendered is what the preview shows: the lights, the environment, the background, the floor, the materials assigned.

2. When a tile stops

Section titled “2. When a tile stops”

Each tile gathers samples until the first of the criteria checked in the Render panel:

Criterion Stops the tile For
Samples At this number of samples per pixel (512) A known quality, a known time per tile
Seconds When the duration of the render is spent A deadline: the time shared by the tiles
Noise % When the noise left is below this percentage (2) Simple scenes done early, glass refined

A number of samples alone is too many for a simple product and too few for glass: the noise adapts each tile to what it shows. It is measured as the samples double (16, 32, 64…): the mean difference of the luminance of the pixels between the two, relative to their luminance. A tile of background stops at 16 samples, a tile of polished metal goes on. With Seconds, the time left is shared by the tiles left: a tile done early gives its time to the next ones. At least one criterion stays checked.

3. The denoiser

Section titled “3. The denoiser”

Once the tiles are done, the à-trous filter of the path tracer smooths the whole image, guided by the colors, the normals and the distances of the surfaces seen, the textures kept sharp. It runs on the whole image, not tile by tile: no seams. It blurs less where the pixels have more samples. Denoiser sets its strength: 1 by default, more for a smoother image, 0 for none.

Tiles sets their side, 256 pixels by default: smaller ones bound the memory and the length of the work given to the GPU at once, larger ones lose less time between tiles.

4. Follow, stop, resume

Section titled “4. Follow, stop, resume”

The Render panel and the right of the status bar say where it is:

  • Render · tile 3 / 40 · 64 samples · noise 3.1 % · 0:12;
  • Denoising · 1:01: the tiles done;
  • Render done · 40 tiles · 32–512 samples · 1:02: the fewest and the most samples a tile gathered;
  • Render stopped · tile 6 / 40, Render failed: ….

Stop the render (or Stop) stops it after its batch of frames; Resume the render (or Resume) starts again at the tile it was rendering, the tiles done kept. Done, the image is shown under the progress; Save the render… writes it: its format, its alpha, its passes and the folder are those of Saving a render.

5. Where no rays are traced

Section titled “5. Where no rays are traced”

A final render needs the path tracer: WebGPU or Vulkan. On WebGL2, the Render panel says it and its button is disabled. Open this page with ?backend=webgl2 to see it.

6. Without the editor

Section titled “6. Without the editor”

FinalRender of @fdw/engine-raytracing renders an image by tiles with a PathTracer (without its own filter: the image is denoised whole). Its denoiser is an interface (Denoiser): the à-trous filter by default (atrousDenoiser), another one in its place (denoiser), or none (null).

In a plugin of the editor, level.render renders the level (its stage, its materials) by a camera; level.render.progress tells where it is, level.render.image gives its image in PNG; level.render.stop stops it, and level.render of the same id resumes it.
