Source editor/editor-ui

The interface of the editor, shared by its hosts: the Electron application (labs/editor-desktop) and the web one (labs/editor-web) (P18 of design/roadmap.md, design in design/editor-ui.md, in French).

The host

Section titled “The host”

An application gives the shell an EditorHost:

Service What Electron (labs/editor-desktop) Web (labs/editor-web)
engine The connection to the engine (EngineConnection) A MessagePort to the utility process A MessageChannel to the engine in the page
Viewport The component of the area where the engine shows a view (view: its id) RemoteViewport, a child window over the area, one per view CanvasViewport, a canvas of the engine in the area, one per view
files Opening and saving the files of the user The dialogs of Electron, read by the main process The File System Access API, else an import and a download
storage JSON documents kept between sessions (the layout), by key Files in userData/storage IndexedDB
popoutUrl The empty page the popouts of the panels open popout.html, the only window the page may open popout.html, subject to the popup blocker
about Shown at the right of the status bar The versions of Electron, Chrome and Node The browser and the platform
plugins The plugins of the application, after those of the editor The sample and isolate plugins, chunks of the bundle The sample and isolate plugins, chunks of the bundle
profile The application on the shell (below): the editor when absent FDW_PROFILE, given to the page by the main process FDW_PROFILE, or ?profile=

The shell is tested with a fake host (fakeHost of @fdw/editor-ui/testing, in jsdom: EditorShell.spec.tsx, and the tests of the plugins), its layout in Chromium with the web host (labs/editor-web).

The profiles

Section titled “The profiles”

An application on the shell is a profile (EditorProfile, profile.ts; design/p26-platform.md §6, in French): the plugins it loads (plugins, by id; all of them when absent), the commands it permits (commands.allow and commands.deny, ids or prefixes ending with *), whether its panels edit (editing), its menu bar (menuBar), its chrome and the views open in a new layout (chrome, views), its title at the left of the menu bar. Several are built in (builtinProfiles, profileNamed): editorProfile, viewerProfile (no editing command, the inspector and the tree read only, none of the plugins of the host, the report of the models, the panels of the measures, the sections and the explosion, the Show menu and toolbars: below), embeddedViewerProfile (viewer-embedded: the view and the toolbars of the viewer, embedded in a page), materialEditorProfile (material-editor: the materials in tabs and their preview, without scene nor viewport, made by @fdw/editor-plugin-material-graph), simulationProfile (simulation: the viewer and the results of a simulation in contours, made by @fdw/editor-plugin-simulation) and configuratorProfile (the viewport alone), among others (designReviewProfile, the embedded editor of materials).

  • A plugin the profile does not list is not registered: its code is never loaded.
  • A command it does not permit is not shown in the menus, the toolbars and the palette, and does not run (CommandService.permitted).
  • Its context keys: profile and profile.editing.
  • Its layout is kept under its own key (profileLayoutKey: layout, layout.viewer…); a reset gives back its chrome.

The viewer

Section titled “The viewer”

The universal viewer (design/p27-viewer.md, in French, steps 1 to 4, 6 and 8) is the viewer profile on the level, not a document of its own: the tree, the selection, the picking and the framing are the level’s.

  • Opening a model (model.open, File → Open a model…, a file dropped on the viewport or the tree, ?open=<url> in the web editor): a file of a format the pivot of P22 reads (EditorEnginePlatform.pivot, glTF, STEP, BREP, FreeCAD and the results of VTK by default; ready.models lists the extensions), opened as the level by LevelDocument.openModel, in four phases (ModelOpening): the file read whole and adapted (pivotLoader of @fdw/engine-pivot, under OpenedModelType), the structure made at once and sent (the nodes pending, LevelNode.pending; the camera framed on the bounds of the meshes on the CPU), the geometry part by part, the largest first and the hidden last, in slices of 8 ms (level.loading: the phase, the parts loaded); the report (level.report, the Report panel; a line in the console with the number of losses). Opened from a URL (model.open with its url), the files it names beside it, the steps of a series, are read there (besideSource); opened from its bytes alone, only the file is known. The refinement at the zoom waits for the tessellation of P23.
  • The selection by instance path (level.selection.paths, selectPaths), at a granularity (level.granularity: assembly, the default, or part; Show → Select assemblies / parts, kept with the layout): a click selects the assembly under the model (its single-child nodes skipped) or the part; a click inside the only node selected goes a level down; Select the parent (Alt+↑) goes up.
  • The view state (ViewState): hide (H), isolate the rest hidden (Shift+H) or ghosted, show all (Alt+H), color; by instance path, inherited down, never in the model nor its history. Drawn by a RenderSource over the scene (document.view.source): hidden items left out (not picked), colored ones with a material of their color, ghosted ones transparent and not picked. The tree shows it (LevelNode.look); in a profile that does not edit, its eye hides in the view.
  • The search of the tree: the nodes whose name holds the text and their parents; Enter selects them all.
  • The measures (LevelMeasures, document.measures; the tools measure.distance, measure.angle, measure.radius, measure.thickness, measure.faceAngle of the viewport, Show → Measure): the main button picks on the meshes on the CPU (what the view shows only: not the hidden, ghosted or cut away), snapping to a vertex or an edge within six pixels; the measures of @fdw/engine-measure, always with their precision, kept as annotations of the view state anchored on their parts; the Measures panel, the status bar (the measure being taken, the last one). Drawn by the overlay of the views (LevelOverlay: MeasureDrawing, the points picked).
  • The sections (view.section, view.sectionBox, view.section.set, view.section.remove; Show → Section, the Sections panel): across an axis through the selection or the level, a section box a little larger than it; moved, flipped, turned off; hatched slices; drawn by the clipping of the renderer and the planes of @fdw/engine-draw. The limits refused with a line of the console.
  • The explosion (view.explode, view.explode.offset, view.explode.reset; Show → Explode, Shift+E, the Explosion panel): the explosion of the view state (radial, axial, by levels, manual from the mode of before), its factor animated (LevelViewState.step), the parts drawn moved by the source of the view state (a world matrix of their own, the frustum widened), traces; never in the model. The measures are picked where the parts are shown and taken where they are, drawn between the points shown with a warning.
  • The view state as JSON (level.viewState, level.loadViewState), and its summary for the page (level.view: the measures, the sections, the explosion, the measure being taken).
  • Embedded (serveViewerApi, viewerCommands, viewerEvents): the API of @fdw/viewer-embed served on the runtime of the shell (open, select, frame, cut, explode, the view state, run a command), on any channel; the web editor serves it to its parent with ?api.
  • The properties (LevelProperties.groups, three sources): the properties and the extensions of the node of the pivot and of its mesh, by namespace (an extension raw, read only); computed ones (measureNode: the volume, the area, the center of mass, the bounds, the triangles, said approximate: on the mesh until the B-rep); the instance (the node of the pivot, its source key, its occurrence, its instances, its world position). The computed ones are given for any node with meshes, in the editor too.

The views

Section titled “The views”

The viewport plugin shows the main view (mainView, viewport) and, split from it (Scene → Split the viewport, fdw.viewport.split), up to three others (viewport.2 to viewport.4), the same scene each with its camera; Scene → Link the cameras (fdw.viewport.link, the context key viewport.linked, kept with the layout) makes their cameras move together, the default. Any plugin shows a view of its own by rendering the Viewport of the host with its id. Each view is a surface of the engine on the same backend: its bounds, its holes and its inputs carry its id (view), view.close destroys it, view.link links its camera; the engine answers view.state (whether the scene shows in it, and why not). A scene shows in other views when it has addView (@fdw/demo-scenes): the level does, the demo scenes have one view.

The layout

Section titled “The layout”

Laid out like a QMainWindow (design/editor-ui.md §3, in French):

  • The workbench (Workbench.tsx) is a GridviewReact of dockview with four areas, the central one and the docks, each a DockviewReact: tabs, groups split by dragging, floating groups in the central area. A tab moves within its area, not to another one.
  • The panels are the views of the plugins (below), each a panel of the component view (ViewHost, views.tsx) with the id of its view; in a new layout, those open in their area, the first one registered active (the editor’s before the host’s): the viewport and a welcome page in the central area, the outliner and the inspector of the level (below), the console (the messages of the engine) and the list of the plugins in the docks. They stay mounted behind another tab and when moved (the always renderer): the viewport keeps its canvas or its window, hidden while its tab is.
  • The menu bar, the toolbars and the status bar (chrome.tsx) are out of dockview, filled by the registries of the plugins. The View menu (the workbench plugin) shows and hides the docks, the panels, the toolbars (on one of the four sides) and the status bar, opens the command palette, and resets the layout; the Scene menu (the viewport plugin) chooses the scene: the level, or a demo scene; the File and Edit menus are the level’s.
  • The layout document (layout.ts, version 2), written by the host under the key of the profile (layout for the editor) once the changes settle: the docks shown, the toolbars and their side, the status bar (the editor store), the sizes of the docks (toJSON of the grid) and the panels of each area (toJSON of each area), the state of the machine and of the plugins (below). A document of another version, or one dockview does not restore, gives the default layout. A panel whose plugin is gone stays, and tells so.
  • The theme of dockview (.dockview-theme-fdw in src/styles.css): the sizes of its dark theme, the colors of shadcn.

The state and the services

Section titled “The state and the services”

Following design/editor-state.md (in French), in src/state. The shell makes the runtime of the editor (EditorRuntime) once the layout is read, and disposes it when it goes; the state of the engine is followed from the start, not to miss its first messages. onRuntime gives it to the host (and to the tests).

  • The services (ServiceRegistry): typed ids (serviceId<T>('name'), get returns a T), made at their first use (lazy: false at once), each with the services of its own scope. A scope (createScope(name, visible?)), for a plugin, sees its services and those of its parent, all or those listed; disposed, it disposes what it registered, the last first (the dispose option, else the dispose() of the service). The runtime registers the host, the connection and the state of the engine, the stores, the machine, the context keys and the registries of the tools and the modes (IEditorHost, IEditorMachine, IContextKeys…). Nothing of it is kept.
  • The stores (@xstate/store): the editor store (createEditorStore), serializable, with the chrome (the docks, the toolbars and their side, the status bar), the selection (ids, those of the level) and the state of each plugin; the engine store (createEngineStore), what the engine tells of itself (the backend, the scenes, the stats, the console), from its messages, never kept; the level store (createLevelStore), the projection of the level (below), never kept either.
  • The machine of the editor (editorMachine, XState 5): fixed, in parallel, the focus of the window, the mode and two slots of tools, primary then secondary by priority. A tool (ToolSpec) or a mode (ModeSpec) of a plugin is a child actor, made from its registry (runtime.tools, runtime.modes) when activated: one tool at a time in a slot, the slots independent. Its logic is given a ToolContext, never the machine: the context keys, done() and cancel(). The core names its guards and actions (toolAllowed, startTool…) and startEditor implements them on the registries, with the when clause of the tool or the mode. Escape (EDITOR.CANCEL) ends every tool; the focus of a view (FOCUS.VIEW, from the area clicked or focused) and the loss of the window (FOCUS.WINDOW) are handled by the core, then forwarded to the tools (EDITOR.VIEW, EDITOR.BLUR). A tool or a mode unregistered ends.
  • The context keys (createContextKeys): derived from the machine and the stores, editor.focusedArea, editor.focusedView, editor.windowFocused, editor.mode, tool.primary, tool.secondary, scene.hasSelection, engine.ready, engine.backend, engine.fallback, engine.scene, level.open, level.dirty, level.canUndo, level.canRedo; a plugin may set its own (keys.set). The same object until a key changes.
  • The when clauses (compileWhen, matchesWhen): as in VS Code, a key alone (truthy), key == value and key != value (a bare value, a number, true, or quoted), !, && before ||, parentheses. A clause that is not valid is false, with a warning.
  • The state kept: the layout document holds, with the chrome and dockview, the snapshot of the machine (persistEditor) and the state of the plugins. XState does not persist an inline child: each tool is persisted on its own, with its id as its source, and restored with the logic of its registry; a tool or a mode no more registered restores as idle, a snapshot that does not restore gives the default state. The selection is not kept: its ids are those of a session of the engine (the project of P20 will keep it).
  • In React (editorContext.tsx): useEditor() gives the runtime, useSelector(store or actor, select) a part of its snapshot, useEngine(), useEditorStore(), useEditorMachine(select), useWhen(clause). The status bar shows the mode and the tools active.

Tested without React (the services, the registries, the clauses, the keys, the machine and its persistence) and in the shell (the focus, the keys, the state kept). The routing of the inputs by the slots: the main button of the viewport goes to the tool of the primary slot (the level, below).

The plugins

Section titled “The plugins”

Following design/editor-ui.md §6 (in French), in src/plugins: the model of VS Code, a manifest and code loaded on demand. The editor has no interface of its own: its panels, its menus, its toolbars and its status bar come from plugins, built in (src/builtins) or given by the host (EditorHost.plugins).

  • The manifest (manifest.ts), checked when the plugin is registered (readManifest): its id, its version, the range of the API it is written for (engine, against editorApiVersion), its contributions. An invalid manifest, or a contribution whose id is taken, makes the plugin invalid, without contributions.
  • The contributions, declared at once, before the code is loaded: the commands (a title, a category, an icon, an enablement and a toggled clause; palette: false for one that needs arguments); the views (their area, open in a new layout, a when for the View menu); the menus by id (menubar, whose items are submenus; the items sorted by group, then by order, separated by group, each with a when); the toolbars (their side and visibility by default, their items, commands); the items of the status bar (a side, a priority; a component of the plugin, or a text that may run a command); the keybindings (ctrl+shift+p, escape, f1; the letters by their place on the keyboard; in a text field only those with ctrl, alt or meta, or escape; the last one registered whose clause holds wins); the tools and the modes, which are commands too (Tool: Pen, run again to end it). The icons are names of editorIcons (lucide), known without the code of the plugin.
  • The registries of the runtime (runtime.commands, views, menus, toolbars, statusBar, keybindings, tools, modes): the shell only reads them.
  • The activation (PluginHost, runtime.plugins): the code is loaded and activate(context) called at the first event that names the plugin: onView:id (its panel shown), onCommand:id (a command run from a menu, a toolbar, a key or the palette), onTool:id, onMode:id, implied by its contributions, and onStartup in its manifest. The plugins of onStartup, and those of the tools and the mode kept in the layout, are loaded before the runtime is made (preparePlugins): their activate registers their tools before the machine restores. So a plugin reads the machine and the context keys at the call (in a command, a component), not in activate: a warning tells it.
  • The context of a plugin (PluginContext): its manifest; its scope of services (context.services, those of the editor and its own); commands.register and execute, views.registerComponent, statusBar.registerComponent, menus.append (items made by code: one per panel, per scene), tools.register, modes.register, keys.set; state, its JSON kept with the layout, and kept when the plugin is gone (usePluginState in React). A registration names a contribution of its manifest, else it throws; each one is in context.subscriptions.
  • The deactivation: deactivate() of the plugin, then what it registered, the last first, and its scope; its tool ends. The Plugins panel lists the plugins and their state; one not built in is disabled there (its panel tells so), and enabled again. Not kept yet.
  • A missing plugin: a panel of the layout whose view no plugin gives stays, and tells its plugin is missing; its tool or its mode restores as idle; its state is kept.
  • The command palette (CommandPalette.tsx, cmdk in a dialog of Radix, a hole over the native viewport): Ctrl+Shift+P or F1, the commands enabled, by their category and title, with their key.
  • In React: useItems(registry), useMenu(id), usePluginState(id), useToolSnapshot(slot) (the state of the tool active in a slot), CommandButton.

The part of a plugin in the engine (P19): an EngineExtension, given to the engine by the host (EditorEnginePlatform.extensions, an EngineExtensionFactory by id), which receives the messages the plugin sends under its id (extension messages, JSON and bytes in data; extensionChannel(connection, id) in the page), sees the scene shown (scene, its graphs), works before each frame (frame; before any viewport too, and in a profile without one: the previews of the editor of materials) and may hold the scene (holdsScene: a debugger stopped, its frames not drawn). Its context gives the backend, post, the pivot of the engine (pivot) and readModel (a model read by the pivot without a scene: its visible meshes on the GPU, placed, its bounds, its losses; readModelMeshes). It is told the model opened in the level once its geometry is loaded (level, an EngineLevelModel: the model of the pivot and its report, the scene, nodesOf a node of the pivot, readResource for what the reader left deferred, changed to tell the views; undefined when another level replaces it), and may draw over each view of the level in its pixels (overlay(width, height): a legend, a time bar) and in the level itself, with its scene and by the camera of each view (items(): lines on the model, the wire of a mesh undeformed); the simulation plugin is made so (@fdw/editor-plugin-simulation). The editors of graphs are made so (@fdw/graph-editor). A view may open beside the panels of its area (split: 'right' or 'below'): the editors of graphs beside the viewport.

The plugins are trusted: they run in the page, with what it can do (sandboxed third-party plugins come later, with the import map that gives them React and this package). The sample plugin, a tool and a panel, is @fdw/editor-plugin-sample.

Tested without React (the manifests, the versions, the keys, the menus, the commands, the activation and the deactivation, the restore of a tool) and in the shell (a panel loaded when shown, a command from its menu and its key, a tool from a toolbar and Escape, the palette, a missing plugin, the Plugins panel).

The level

Section titled “The level”

The document the editor edits (P16), with the tools of P15 (design/editor-state.md §2, in French): it lives in the engine, which may run in another process; the page holds its projection, by ids, and changes it by commands only.

  • The document (LevelDocument): a new one is the sample level (a ground, cubes, spheres, a table of five nodes, the sun), made only of meshes and materials made in code; another is a file of the user, opened by the host (level.open): a scene of the engine (.scene.json, its buffers in it) or glTF (.glb, .gltf with its buffers in it), read by the store of the levels (installSceneIo, installGltfScenes; the image codecs of the platform, EditorEnginePlatform.imageCodecs). Its references to other files are not found (a level of the user has no folder yet). Saved in one file, written by the engine (level.file) and saved by the host (files.save): the scene of the engine (writeFdwScene, its buffers embedded, the stable ids of its nodes), or exported as a .glb (writeGltf, the instances expanded). Its components without a codec are kept, and saved again.
  • The projection (level.* messages, createLevelStore): the tree whole when the level is opened (level then level.nodes with reset), then the changes grouped by batch of SceneEvents (one command, one step of a drag): the nodes changed (and the parents whose children changed), the ids removed. A node is entity:generation: never the id of another one. The selection (the active node last), the properties of the active node (name, visibility, transform with the rotation in degrees, stable id, components and their fields as their description says, editableFields of @fdw/engine-editing; read only without one), the labels of the next undo and redo, the rectangle of a selection being dragged.
  • The commands (level.command): undo, redo, delete, add (an empty node, a cube, a sphere, a point light), edit a property (consecutive edits of a field make one entry; a field of a component by a FieldCommand on every selected node with the component, a refused value said in the console), move under another node, select (not in the history), frame. Each one is a command of P15 in the history of the level; a drag of the viewport is cancelled first.
  • The viewport (LevelView, the scene level of the Scene menu, the first one by default): the secondary button turns the camera, the middle one pans, the wheel zooms; the main button is the tools’ (EditingTools: click and rectangle select the node under the root, the handles of the gizmo drag), as the page routes it: the tool of the primary slot (level.tool): none, select; Move, Rotate, Scale, the gizmo; another one (a tool of a plugin), nothing. The keys are the page’s (the keybindings below), never read by the engine. The rectangle is drawn by the page, four lines that are holes of the native viewport.
  • The plugin fdw.level (built in): the File menu (new, open, save with Ctrl+S, export as glTF), the Edit menu (undo and redo with Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y, delete, Add, frame with F, the tools Q, W, E, R, the axes of the gizmo X, its pivot C, snapping), its toolbar, the name of the level in the status bar (• when changed). The settings of the gizmo are the state of the plugin, kept with the layout, and context keys (level.gizmo.local, level.gizmo.origins, level.gizmo.snapping).
  • The outliner (fdw.outliner): the tree, the parents of the selection opened; a click selects (Shift adds, Ctrl toggles), a double click frames, the eye shows and hides, a node dragged onto another goes under it. The inspector (fdw.inspector): the properties of the active node, each field validated by Enter or by leaving it; the fields of the components generated from their description (a slider between bounds, a checkbox, a color, groups, units).

Tested without React (LevelDocument: the projection, the commands undone and redone, the components, saved and opened again in both formats; EditorEngine: its messages), in the shell (the tree, the inspector, opening and saving through the host, the routing by the tool of the primary slot, the rectangle) and in Chromium (a rectangle in the viewport picks, the tree selects, Delete and Ctrl+Z).

The engine side

Section titled “The engine side”
  • The protocol (@fdw/editor-ui/protocol): the messages between the page and the engine, the bounds of the viewport, the scene, the inputs. The shell never holds an object of the engine: the engine may run in another process.
  • EditorEngine (@fdw/editor-ui/engine, without React): the engine side of the protocol, independent of where it runs. Its statistics, once a second (stats): the frames per second, the mean CPU time of a frame (the CPU of the process where the platform gives cpuTime: a swapchain waits for the display inside the frame), the GPU time of the passes of the level (timestamps, where the backend has them), shown in the status bar. It receives the messages of the page, shows the scenes in the viewport and sends back what it does; it holds the level (above). Its platform (EditorEnginePlatform) gives it the backend, the viewport, the surface, the scenes and the pace of the frames: the utility process of Electron (labs/editor-desktop/src/engine) on Vulkan, the page of the web editor (labs/editor-web) on WebGPU. Where the engine runs in the page, the platform also gives the inputs (a WebInputSource on the viewport), and the scenes read them without a relay.
  • startBackend (@fdw/editor-ui/engine): the choice of the backend by FDW_BACKEND, the same in every host: the preferred one without the variable (Vulkan, WebGPU), the fallback when the one asked for does not start or is unknown (Dawn, WebGL2), with the reason. The ready message gives the backend running and the reason of a fallback, shown in the status bar.
  • useViewportBounds: the bounds of the area of a view in device pixels, read at each animation frame and sent to the engine when they change, empty while the area is hidden (behind another tab): it places its window over the area (Electron), or sizes its canvas to it (web). A view other than the main one is closed once unmounted (view.close).
  • The other views (EditorEnginePlatform.createView): a child window and its swapchain (Electron: Vulkan, Direct3D 12; Dawn reads each frame back), a canvas (web: WebGPU configures it on the same device; WebGL2 renders on an offscreen canvas and copies each frame to the canvas of its view). The engine makes a view at its first bounds, adds it to the scene shown (Scene.addView) once it has a surface, draws it after the main one, and tells the page when the scene cannot show in it (a scene of one view, a host without createView).
  • RemoteViewport and RemoteInputSource: the two ends of the inputs when the engine is out of the page. The viewport sends the bounds of its area in device pixels, the holes of the page over it, and the inputs read on it by a WebInputSource; the input source replays them in the engine.

The components

Section titled “The components”

shadcn/ui on Tailwind 4, in src/components/ui (an xs size added for the bars of the editor), with the theme in src/styles.css: the application’s Vite runs Tailwind (@tailwindcss/vite), the dark class on <html> sets the dark theme. The components are copied from shadcn with relative imports. The menu bar is the Menubar of shadcn (Radix). Nothing of the page is drawn above the native viewport of Electron: the popups of Radix, the floating groups and the drop targets of dockview, and the elements marked data-over-viewport, are holes in its window (viewportHoles, sent by RemoteViewport). The native selects open above it anyway.

React is a peer dependency: the application gives its own (resolve.dedupe in Vite).
