Editing a level

This tutorial goes through the editor with the level it opens first: a ground, cubes, spheres, a table and the sun. Everything happens in the editor below; nothing is kept when the page is left, so try freely.

Open the editor in a page of its own

1. The layout

Section titled “1. The layout”

The editor is laid out like the main window of a desktop application:

  • The central area holds the viewport. Its tab can be split or moved in the area, and a group can float above it.
  • The docks hold the panels: the Outliner (the tree of the level) on the left, the Inspector on the right, the Console (the messages of the engine) at the bottom; next to the Inspector, the Statistics of the plugin of Writing a plugin (the Marks menu is that of the sample plugin). Drag a tab to reorder it, or to split its dock. The buttons at the left of the top toolbar show and hide the docks; the View menu shows the panels, the toolbars, and resets the layout.
  • The status bar tells the backend and the device, the frames per second with the CPU and GPU time of a frame, the size of the viewport, the name of the level (• once changed), the mode and the tools active.
  • The command palette (Ctrl+Shift+P or F1) lists every command enabled, with its key: type a few letters of its name.

The editor keeps its layout between sessions (the desktop one in its user data, the web one in IndexedDB); not in the pages of this site, which start from the default one.

2. Moving around

Section titled “2. Moving around”

The camera turns around a point of the level:

Input What it does
Right button, dragged Turns around the point
Middle button, dragged Pans
Wheel Zooms
F Frames the selection (Edit → Frame)

A drag goes on outside of the viewport: the pointer stays captured until the button is released.

3. Selecting

Section titled “3. Selecting”

The main button belongs to the tool of the editor (below). With any of them:

  • a click selects the object under the pointer, a click in the void clears the selection; Shift adds to it, Ctrl toggles, Alt removes;
  • a drag from the void draws a rectangle: the objects inside are selected;
  • in the Outliner, a click selects too (Shift, Ctrl as well), a double click frames, the eye hides and shows.

A click in the viewport selects a whole object under the root: the table, not one of its legs. To reach a leg, open the table in the Outliner and click the leg there.

The selected objects are outlined, the one under the pointer too, more faintly. The active object is the last selected: the Inspector shows it.

4. Moving, turning, scaling

Section titled “4. Moving, turning, scaling”

The tools are in the top toolbar and in the Edit menu:

Key Tool The gizmo
Q Select None: the main button selects only
W Move Arrows along the axes, squares for the planes
E Rotate Rings around the axes
R Scale Handles along the axes, the center for all three

Select the blue cube, press W, and drag its red arrow: the cube moves along X. Each drag is one step of the history. Three toggles change the gizmo:

  • X: the axes of the world, or those of the object;
  • C: the pivot, at the center of the selection, or at the origin of each object;
  • the magnet: snapping, by half a meter, 15 degrees, a quarter of scale.

5. The Inspector

Section titled “5. The Inspector”

The Inspector edits the active object: its name, its visibility, its position, rotation (in degrees) and scale, and its components. A field is applied by Enter, or by leaving it; the consecutive changes of one field make one step of the history. Id is the stable id of the object, saved with the level.

The fields of a component are generated from its description (Describing a component): labels and units, a slider between bounds, a checkbox, a color, the fields grouped. A component without a description is shown read only. A field changed with several objects selected changes it on each of them that has the component, in one step; a value out of the bounds is refused, and the Console says why.

6. Adding, removing, reorganizing

Section titled “6. Adding, removing, reorganizing”
  • Edit → Add adds an empty node, a cube, a sphere or a point light at the center of the level, selected: move it with W.
  • Delete removes the selection.
  • In the Outliner, drag an object onto another to put it under it: it moves with its new parent.

7. Undoing

Section titled “7. Undoing”

Every change is a command of the history: Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes. The Edit menu tells the next step of each. Selecting is not in the history, as in most editors.

8. Saving and opening

Section titled “8. Saving and opening”

The File menu:

  • Save (Ctrl+S) writes the level as a scene of the engine (.scene.json, its buffers in it), the format of saving scenes, with the stable ids of the objects;
  • Export as glTF writes a .glb, for the other tools;
  • Open (Ctrl+O) reads a scene of the engine or a glTF file (.glb, or .gltf with its buffers in it);
  • New goes back to the sample level.

In the browser, Chromium opens a dialog to choose where to write; the other browsers download the file. On the desktop, the dialogs are those of the system.

9. Rendering with rays

Section titled “9. Rendering with rays”

Edit → Rendering chooses how the viewport draws the level: Rasterized (the default), Ray traced shadows, Ray traced reflections (the shadows too), or Path traced: the whole image by rays, clearing up while the camera stays still (see ray tracing). The command palette has them too. The renderings by rays need a backend that traces them: Vulkan on the desktop, WebGPU in the browser; on WebGL2 they are disabled. The choice is kept with the layout. Selecting, the outlines and the gizmos work in every rendering.

10. The other scenes

Section titled “10. The other scenes”

The Scene menu shows the demo scenes in the viewport instead of the level: physics, the ocean, the game in the forest… They run with the inputs of the game: click the viewport, then play. Scene → level comes back to the level, as it was left.
