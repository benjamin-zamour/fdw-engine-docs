A first person shooter

A second game, in first person this time. The player stands in a walled arena, at the end of the day; drones come in through the four gates, in waves, and fly at the player to strike it. The weapon in the hand shoots them down, three hits each. Each wave is bigger than the last; the game ends when the health of the player falls to zero.

The game reuses what the maze built — static nodes, a tag Solid, the collisions of circles, unlit materials, components with columns, a scheduler — and adds what a first person game needs: a camera at the height of the eyes, the pointer locked, a weapon that follows the view, shots traced with rays, enemies drawn between two steps.

…

Click the arena, then Play: the mouse looks around, W A S D walks, Shift runs, Space jumps, a click (or F, held for automatic fire) shoots. Escape or P opens the menu: the arrows choose, Enter or a click confirms. With a gamepad: the left stick to walk, the right stick to look, the south button to jump, the right trigger to shoot, Start for the menu.

Where the code is

Section titled “Where the code is”

The code of the game is in labs/demo-scenes/src/fps/, shared by:

  • the browser demo: npx nx serve demo, then the FPS route;
  • the native demo: npx nx run demo-native:start -- fps (Escape quits the native demo: P pauses there);
  • this page.
File Contents

1. The arena: a static level

Section titled “1. The arena: a static level”

The arena is 40 m across, centered on the origin. Everything in it is built once, and never moves: every node is static, indexed once in the octree of the scene.

  • The floor is made of tiles of 2 m, in two shades: a scale for the eye, to feel the speed of walking. The tiles cast no shadow, and are not solid.
  • The walls are 4 m high, with a gate of 3 m in the middle of each, and a dark recess behind it: the drones come out of the dark.
  • Eight pillars in a ring and a score of crates give cover. The crates are placed by a seeded random generator, away from the four lanes from the gates: the drones always find their way in.
  • What blocks is Solid, as in the maze: the player and the drones collide with the same entities. Marked occluder too, the walls, the pillars and the crates are ready for the occlusion culling: they hide what is behind them.

The light is a low sun, casting long shadows across the floor, and four lamps on posts at the corners — point lights, with a glowing bulb each. The bulbs use the unlit glowMaterial of the maze (step 4): their color does not depend on the lights. The game uses it for everything that glows — the bulbs, the eyes of the drones, the muzzle flash, the sparks, the crosshair.

2. Two contexts of actions

Section titled “2. Two contexts of actions”

The game reads two ActionMaps, as in First person controls: one for the game, one for the menu. The game starts in the menu, so its map starts disabled.

  • FlyActions are the actions the FlyOperator reads: move, look, run, lift. The game takes them all, and empties lift: a walker does not fly up and down. Bound to nothing, the axis stays at 0.
  • locked is a button down while the pointer is locked. In a browser, Escape is the key that releases the lock: the game watches locked being released, and opens the menu then.

3. The player: a camera at the height of the eyes

Section titled “3. The player: a camera at the height of the eyes”

The player is no body seen from afar: it is the node of the camera. A FlyOperator in walk mode turns it with the mouse or the right stick, and walks it horizontally, wherever it looks:

The operator moves a CameraTarget: an object with a position, a rotation and a projection. Here, its position and rotation are those of the transform of the node, and its projection that of the camera — the operator reads the aspect, so that the pointer turns the view as much in both directions.

The operator moves the eye; the player then corrects it. Each frame:

  • The jump is a vertical speed, pulled down by gravity, until the feet are back at 0. grounded forbids a second jump in the air.
  • The collisions are those of the maze (step 2): a circle of 40 cm, pushed out of the solid boxes near it, sliding along them. The circle is tested at the height of the knees, not of the eyes: a crate of 1 m stops the player, as a wall does. The circle rises with the feet: at the top of a jump, about 95 cm up, it passes over a small crate.
  • The gates lead out, into the recesses: the position is also clamped to the inside of the arena.
  • fly.apply() writes the position and the angles back into the node, after the corrections.

The recoil. At each shot, the view kicks up a little, then comes back down, slower:

The recoil left is remembered apart: only what the shots added is taken back, while the player keeps aiming with the mouse in between.

4. The weapon in the hand

Section titled “4. The weapon in the hand”

The weapon is a few cubes — a body, a barrel, a grip, a glowing sight — children of the node of the camera. The hierarchy does the rest: the weapon follows every movement of the view, without a line of code.

  • About 25 cm from the eye, the weapon needs a camera that sees close: its near is 5 cm. The muzzle is 46 cm ahead, the player keeps 40 cm from the walls: against a wall, the barrel sinks into it.
  • The weapon casts no shadow: it is part of the view, not of the arena.
  • Each frame, it is placed in the lower right of the view, swaying with the steps — the distance walked, not the time, so it is still when the player stops — and kicked back after a shot.

The flash. At the muzzle, a glowing ball and a point light, hidden. A shot shows them for 50 ms: the light lights the weapon, and the walls close by.

The crosshair is a small cube, also a child of the eye, half a meter ahead, in the center of the view. A drone hit turns it red and bigger for a moment: its node gets another material.

What the player carries is on its own layer

Section titled “What the player carries is on its own layer”

The weapon, the crosshair and the sparks are on a layer of their own, ViewLayer. The renderer draws every layer; the shots search all the others:

Without it, the first thing every shot would hit is the weapon, or the crosshair right in front of the eye, or the spark of the previous shot.

5. The drones: a component, and its systems

Section titled “5. The drones: a component, and its systems”

Each drone is a node — a body, a red eye, two wings, children of its node — and an entity with a drone component:

The logic of the drones runs at the fixed step, in three systems that go through the columns:

  • steer flies each drone towards the player, at 3.2 m/s, pushed away from the drones closer than 1.6 m, out of the walls with the same resolveCircle as the player, bobbing up and down. Close enough, it stops and hovers.
  • strike: a drone within 1.5 m of the player strikes it, for 12 points, then waits 1.2 s.
  • fade: the white of a hit fades; a drone shot down falls, then its node is removed.

Drawn between two steps

Section titled “Drawn between two steps”

The logic moves the drones 60 times per second; the screen may draw 144 frames. Moved only at the steps, they would stutter. steer keeps the position of the previous step (px, py, pz) before moving, and the frame draws each drone between the two:

alpha, from the scheduler, tells where the frame is between the last two steps, from 0 to 1. The columns are the truth of the logic; the nodes, what is drawn. Systems and the fixed step details the technique.

6. Shooting: a ray from the eyes

Section titled “6. Shooting: a ray from the eyes”

The trigger held and the weapon ready, a shot is a ray from the eyes, along the view, to the first thing it meets:

  • button('fire').down, not pressed: the trigger held fires again as soon as the weapon is ready — automatic fire, at the rate of period.
  • The hits come nearest first: the first one stops the walk. A crate between the player and a drone takes the shot.
  • forward is the direction of the view, from the angles of the operator: the ray goes through the crosshair.

The ray hits a part of a drone — its body, its eye, a wing — not the node of the drone, which has no mesh. The drone is the parent of the part:

Then:

  • A drone hit flashes white (its body gets the glowing material for 80 ms), the crosshair turns red; at the third hit, it is shot down, and the score grows by 100 times the number of the wave.
  • The arena hit: a spark flies 5 cm before the surface, not inside it. The sparks are twelve glowing cubes, reused: the oldest moves to the new hit, shrinks and spins, then hides.
  • Hit or miss, the view kicks up, and the gamepad rumbles: a little for a miss, more for a hit, most for a drone shot down.

7. The frame, with a scheduler

Section titled “7. The frame, with a scheduler”

fps.ts puts everything into the four phases of a Scheduler:

Two choices differ from the maze:

  • The player is not at the fixed step. The view must follow the mouse at every frame: turned at 60 steps per second on a 144 Hz screen, it would judder at each movement of the hand. Walking goes with it, and the collisions are tested each frame, with the time of the frame — at most 0.1 s, as frame caps it.
  • The shots come after draw. The ray tests the nodes where they are drawn, between two steps: the player hits what they see, not where the logic was a fraction of a step ago.

8. The menu and the pause

Section titled “8. The menu and the pause”

The menu is open at the start, at a pause, at the end of a game. Opening it switches the contexts, pauses the scheduler and releases the pointer:

  • scheduler.paused stops the fixed phase only: under the menu, the drones freeze, and the frame goes on — the input, the menu, the rendering.
  • play is called from a click or a key: a browser only locks the pointer in answer to a gesture of the user.
  • The menu has four entries: Play (Resume, Play again), the rumble, the inverted vertical axis, the sensitivity of the pointer. These settings are properties of the game and of the operator: game.rumble, fly.invertY, fly.pointerSpeed.

When the health of the player falls to 0, the strikes system ends the game and opens the menu, at once: the steps left in the frame are dropped.

The view tells the state. The exposure of the rendering dims the arena under the menu, and darkens it for a moment at each blow:

The line under the canvas gives the wave, the drones flying, the health, the score and the hits; under the menu, its entries, the selected one in brackets.

9. The waves

Section titled “9. The waves”

When no drone is left — the last one fallen and removed — the game waits two seconds, then sends the next wave:

The drones come in through the gates in turn — north first, ahead of the player at the start — three abreast at most in each. Their phases differ, so they do not bob together.

10. The sounds

Section titled “10. The sounds”

The game tells what happens — a shot, a hit, a drone shot down, a blow, a wave — to an FpsSoundEvents, given when there is an audio engine (context.audio: a WebAudioEngine in the page, a NativeAudioEngine in the native demo). FpsSounds plays them on the audio of the engine:

  • The effects (the shot, the tick of a hit, the blow) are SoundEffects: a pitch drawn at each play, a number of voices bounded.
  • The drones hum where they fly: a voice per drone, attached to its node, heard from the eye; the hum fades out when the drone falls, and an explosion rings where it is.
  • The music is a MusicPlayer: a calm loop under the menu, crossfaded to a faster one in the game, on a music bus at half the volume.
  • The menu has a Sound entry: the master bus muted.

Every sound is made by code (demo-scenes/src/audio/synth.ts: noise, sines, envelopes, a small sequencer for the music): nothing to load, the same in the browser and in the native demo. In the page, the sound starts at the first click.

Testing it

Section titled “Testing it”

The game runs without a page and without a GPU: FpsGame takes a scene, an arena, a camera and an ActionMap, and a FakeInputSource drives the actions. fps/fps.spec.ts walks the player into a wall, makes it jump, shoots a drone down in three hits, shoots at the floor without hitting what the player carries, lets the drones strike until the game is over, and checks that a wave down brings a bigger one. On a FakeAudioEngine, it checks that each drone hums where it flies, that the shots and hits play, that a drone shot down explodes in the world, and that the music crossfades when the game starts.

What we have

Section titled “What we have”
  • A level of static nodes, solid and occluding, lit by a low sun and lamps.
  • A player that is a camera: a FlyOperator in walk mode, corrected for the jump and the collisions.
  • A weapon made of children of the camera, on a layer of its own, that the shots do not see.
  • Drones as entities, moved by systems at the fixed step, drawn between two steps.
  • Shots traced with rays of the scene, from the parts hit back to the drone.
  • A pause menu: two contexts of actions, the pointer locked and released, the scheduler paused.
  • Sounds made by code: effects varied at each play, drones humming in space, a music crossfaded between the menu and the game.

Some ideas left for later: drones that shoot back, with rays of their own; a weapon drawn in a pass of its own, so that it never sinks into a wall; a minimap, as in the maze.
