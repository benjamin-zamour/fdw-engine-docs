A third-person game

A third game, seen over the shoulder. A ranger stands in a clearing of a fir forest, under a low sun; orcs come out of the trees, in waves, then a demon leads the last one. The ranger strikes with an estoc — three blows in a row, or a heavy one — parries, rolls out of the way, jumps. The game is won when the demon falls, lost when the ranger does.

The game puts together most of the engine: the forest is instanced, with its levels of detail (P1), under shadows in cascades (P2), on a terrain of patches (P3b), its trunks hiding what is behind them (P3); the camera follows the ranger over its shoulder, the ranger is a character controller (P4, P7b); the models and their animations come from glTF files (P5, P6), the sounds are made by code (P7). It adds what a fight needs: blows as volumes tested while the animations strike, and enemies decided by a behavior graph (P9).

…

Click the forest, then Play: the mouse turns the camera, W A S D (Z Q S D on AZERTY) moves, Shift sprints, a click strikes (click again for the next blow), the right button or E strikes hard, F held parries, C rolls, Space jumps. Escape or P opens the menu. With a gamepad: the sticks to move and turn, the west button to strike, the north one for a heavy blow, the left shoulder to parry, the east button to roll, the south one to jump, Start for the menu.

Where the code is

Section titled “Where the code is”

The code of the game is in labs/demo-scenes/src/tps/, shared by:

  • the browser demo: npx nx serve demo, then the TPS route;
  • the native demo: npx nx run demo-native:start -- tps (P pauses there, Escape quits);
  • this page.
File Contents
assets.ts Loading the models and the textures, with the progress
forest.ts The terrain, the firs and the ground cover, their levels and bodies
level.ts The forest as a level of the engine: saved once, loaded by the game
actor.ts A character: a controller, a model turned where it faces, animated
hero.ts The ranger: its actions, its animation graph, its blade
combat.ts The blows: volumes struck once per blow
brain.ts The behavior graph of the enemies, and the nodes of the game it calls
enemies.ts The orcs and the demon: their bodies, what the nodes of the graph do
controls.ts The actions of the game and of the menu
sounds.ts The sounds, made by code
tps.ts The SceneFactory: the menu, the waves, the frame run by a scheduler

1. The models

Section titled “1. The models”

Every model is CC0: free to use, change and share, without credit.

Model Author Used for
Fir Tree 01, Fern 02, Grass Medium 01, Forest Ground 04 Poly Haven The forest, the ground
Antique Estoc Poly Haven The sword
Modular Character Outfits – Fantasy (Male Ranger) Quaternius The ranger
Universal Animation Library 1 and 2 Quaternius The animations of the ranger
Ultimate Monsters (Orc, Demon) Quaternius The enemies

They are not used as downloaded: the fir has 4 million triangles, the ranger textures of 4096 px, its animations are in other files. Two tools prepare them into compact .glb files, in apps/docs/public/models/forest/ (26 MB in all):

  • tools/assets/make-forest.mjs: the firs in three levels of detail (see Levels of detail), the ferns and the grass simplified, made opaque (their leaves are geometry, not textures with alpha);
  • tools/assets/make-characters.mjs: the ranger with its textures reduced, the clips it plays copied from the animation libraries by the names of the joints (the same skeleton), and the estoc in its right hand. Where the estoc sits is computed from the pose of the hand in the guard of Sword_Idle: the grip through the middle phalanges, the blade out of the thumb side, the guard along the fingers.

2. The forest

Section titled “2. The forest”

buildForest makes the level, from a seed:

  • A terrain of 512 m, drawn by patches as in Tessellation, with heights of its own: a flat clearing, gentle bumps (a fbm of @fdw/engine-noise), and around the play area a rim too steep for the character to climb (maxSlope of 42°). The ground is the forest floor of Poly Haven, repeated every 4 m, its normal map over the normal of the height map; its repetition broken by noises of the world position (patches greener or drier, the scan read at a second scale); on the slopes, a rock baked from noises while the models load, read in triplanar mapping (see A procedural terrain).
  • 2 500 firs, at least 6 m apart, of three kinds, turned and scaled at random. Each kind, each level, each primitive is a single scene.createBatch for all its firs; SceneLods chooses their levels for the camera (30 m, 80 m, 320 m). The trunks are occluders.
  • Grass and ferns, about 5 000 clumps, drawn within 30 to 40 m of the camera (a single level each).
  • Their bodies: the terrain as a heightfield, from the same heights; the trunks near the play area as capsules.

The forest is built once, by tools/assets/make-tps-level.ts, and saved as a level of the engine beside the models: forest/forest.scene.json (the nodes, the batches and their levels of detail, the bodies, the terrain and its material) and its binary (the transforms, the heights). The game loads it (createTpsScene({ level: TpsLevelPath })) instead of building it: the same forest, the same game, played the same. The terrain and what the game needs of the forest are two components of the demo, saved by codecs of its own (see Saving and loading scenes); the rock baked at the start is defined in the store under the URI the level names (defineTexture). With --gltf, the tool writes the forest in glTF too, for other tools.

The renderer draws the scene and the terrain together, in about 40 calls for the view and 200 with the four cascades:

The sun has four cascades up to 110 m (Shadow cascades and fog); a fog falling with the height hides the end of the forest, and what the levels of detail leave out.

3. A character: Actor

Section titled “3. A character: Actor”

The ranger and the enemies are Actors: a body moved by a character controller, its node under the root (drawn between two steps by ScenePhysics), the model under it, turned where the character faces, skinned and animated.

move(step, velocity, offset) adds the gravity and moves the controller at the fixed step, by an offset more (what the animation moved it); face(direction, step) turns the model; animate(dt), at each frame, plays the animator and poses the skin, applyPose(pose) poses it as given (a ragdoll). The characters are in their own collision group (4), the level in group 1: the ragdolls (group 2) fall on the level, and the characters and the camera go through them.

4. The ranger: one action at a time

Section titled “4. The ranger: one action at a time”

The ranger does one thing at a time: move, air, roll, attackA, attackB, attackC, a recovery after the first two blows, heavy, block, hit, dead. The game code decides the action; the animation graph follows it, by a parameter:

The rules stay in code, where they are easy to read and to test:

  • The chain of blows. A click during a blow queues the next one, which starts once the blade has struck (60 % of the clip); without it, the blow ends in its recovery, which moving or striking cuts short.
  • Facing the enemy. As a blow starts, the ranger turns towards the enemy nearest in front within 4.5 m, else where the camera looks.
  • The lunge. A blow carries the ranger forward by root motion: the clips of the library strike in place, so withLunges writes a step forward in the track of their root joint (35 to 70 cm, eased), and the animator takes it out of the pose (rootMotion: { joint: 'root' }, see Root motion). The movement of each frame, turned where the ranger faces, is added to the next move of the controller: the lunge follows the blend of the clips, their speed, their cross-fades.
  • The roll carries the ranger 4 m; for most of it, nothing hurts it.
  • The parry takes 85 % of a blow off, from the front only.

5. The blows

Section titled “5. The blows”

The clips tell when the blade strikes: events added to them, at the moments the hand moves fastest (measured on the clips):

Between strike and strikeEnd, at each fixed step, the blade is a capsule from the guard to the tip, tested against the bodies of the enemies with world.overlap; the sweep of the tip since the previous step is tested too: a fast blade crosses a body between two steps.

A Blow remembers what it struck: a target is hit once per blow, however many steps the blade stays in it. The enemies strike the same way, with a sphere in front of them.

6. The enemies: a behavior graph

Section titled “6. The enemies: a behavior graph”

Each enemy is an Actor and an entity; what it does, a behavior graph decides (see Behavior graphs): the same graph for the orcs and the demon, an instance per enemy, their differences in the variables given when attached.

At each fixed step, event.update advances its clocks, then a switch on its state variable decides: spawn (a roar), chase, attack, recover, hurt, dead. It goes to attack when the hero is within reach, its cooldown over, and fewer than two strike already; the others wait. Then it steers (enemy.steer), and strikes during the window of its blow (enemy.strike). A blow of the ranger is an event, hit, with its damage: the health drops, the enemy falls, or staggers if the blow beats its poise — the orcs at every blow, the demon at the heavy ones.

The graph decides; the game does the work its nodes ask for (brain.ts declares them, Enemies implements them):

  • enemy.steer: towards the hero, or for those waiting, towards their own place on a ring around it, along a path on the navigation mesh of the forest (a NavAgent each, a new path when the goal moved by 75 cm, three times a second at most); the velocity it would like, given to the crowd. Those striking hold their ground (avoids: false): the others go around them.
  • enemy.die: the body leaves the physics, and a ragdoll takes over from the animation, pushed away from the blow: 13 bodies on the rig of the monsters, the limbs held in cones, the feet (moved by inverse kinematics, under the root of the rig) hung on the lower legs.
  • enemy.strike: the blow, a sphere in front of the enemy, against the hero.
  • enemy.heroDistance, enemy.canAttack, enemy.swing, enemy.sink, enemy.remove: the distance, the place among those striking, the sounds, the fallen sinking into the ground, then gone.

After the graph, a system moves them all: crowd.update(step) gives each a velocity that runs into no other, as close as can be to the one it wanted (see Crowds and obstacles), and the controllers move.

The navigation mesh is built once, when the forest is ready: the ground of the terrain (its height function), the trunks of the play area as capsules, the floors of a polygon within 30 cm of each other — some 16 000 polygons for the play area of 100 m, in about 0.4 s. A path across it takes about a millisecond. The waves come from places on the mesh, not in a trunk.

The graph is compiled to JavaScript; createTpsScene({ behavior: 'interpret' }) has it interpreted, for its debugger. Both cost about 0.2 ms a frame for a wave, mostly in the moves of the character controllers.

7. The camera

Section titled “7. The camera”

A ThirdPersonOperator (see The third-person camera), 4.2 m behind the ranger, over its shoulder. Its probe casts a sphere in the physics: the trunks, the ground and the enemies stop it, never the ranger itself.

8. The frame

Section titled “8. The frame”

The buttons pressed are kept until the next fixed step: a frame may run none, or several. The menu pauses the fixed phase, as in the FPS.

9. The sounds

Section titled “9. The sounds”

Made by code, as those of the FPS: the steps on the forest floor, the blade cutting the air, the blows, the parries (steel on steel), the grunts of the orcs and the cry of the demon (a buzzing voice through two formants), where they happen; the wind and the birds of the forest when calm, crossfaded to a music when enemies are around.

Testing it

Section titled “Testing it”

tps/tps.spec.ts tests the parts without a GPU: the heights of the forest (a flat clearing, a rim), the blows (struck once, the capsule along the blade), the graph and the clips of the ranger, its lunges (taken out as root motion under an armature turned and scaled), the bones of the ragdolls of the monsters, the actions, the graph of the enemies on a fake game (the roar, the chase, the blow in its window, two striking at once, the poise, the fall), the sounds on a FakeAudioEngine, and the progress shown while loading.

What we have

Section titled “What we have”
  • A forest of 2 500 instanced firs and 5 000 clumps of grass, with their levels of detail, shadows in cascades and fog, at 60 frames per second and more.
  • Models prepared from CC0 assets by tools: levels made of cards, clips copied by the names of the joints, a sword placed by the pose of a hand.
  • A third-person camera that stops at the trunks.
  • A ranger whose actions are decided by code and followed by its animation graph; blows as volumes, struck once per blow.
  • Enemies decided by a behavior graph, compiled to JavaScript: surrounding the hero, striking two at a time, walking their paths on a navigation mesh around the trees, making way for each other.
  • Blows that carry the ranger by root motion, and enemies falling as ragdolls where the blow sends them.

Some ideas left for later: getting up from a ragdoll (Pose.blend back to an animation), a ragdoll for the ranger, obstacles placed in the forest (a tiled mesh).
