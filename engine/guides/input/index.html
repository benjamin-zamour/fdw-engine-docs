Input

Three packages read the keyboard, the pointer and the gamepads, the same way in a page and in a native window:

Package Contents
@fdw/engine-input The state of the devices (Input), the actions (ActionMap)
@fdw/engine-input-web WebInputSource: the DOM events, the Gamepad API
@fdw/engine-input-native NativeInputSource: a NativeWindow, the gamepads of SDL3

The core depends on neither the DOM nor SDL: an application chooses its source, and the rest of its code is the same everywhere.

A source, an input, a frame

Section titled “A source, an input, a frame”
  • WebInputSource(target) listens to an element — a canvas: give it a tabindex so that it receives the keys once clicked — or to the whole window.
  • NativeInputSource(window) listens to a NativeWindow. Its events are read by runLoop, as the other events of the window.

update() applies the events received since the previous frame, reads the gamepads, then the state does not change until the next update(): all the code of a frame sees the same input.

  • pressed and released are true for the frame of the press or the release. A press shorter than a frame — down and up between two updates — is not lost: pressed is true, isDown false.
  • The repeats of a key held down are not new presses.
  • When the page or the window loses the focus, everything is released: a key released elsewhere does not stay down.
  • With a fixed step, a press is seen by every step of its frame: act on it once per frame, outside of the steps.

The keyboard: positions, not letters

Section titled “The keyboard: positions, not letters”

Keys are named by their position on the keyboard, as the code of the DOM: KeyW, ArrowUp, ShiftLeft, Space, Digit1. On an AZERTY keyboard, the key KeyW is Z: a game bound to KeyW, KeyA, KeyS, KeyD is played with ZQSD there, without configuration. In a native window, SDL gives the same positions (its scancodes), named the same way.

To show a key to the user, input.keyboard.label('KeyW') gives what it produces in the layout of the keyboard (Z), once it was pressed — its code before.

The pointer

Section titled “The pointer”

input.pointer gives x and y from 0 to 1 over the surface, from the top left corner, moved during the frame, the buttons (isDown(0): 0 is the main button, 1 the middle, 2 the secondary), dx and dy: how far it moved during the frame, in fractions of the surface (0 before its first move), and wheel: the notches turned during the frame, positive downwards.

input.lockPointer() locks the pointer — hidden, its movements relative and unbounded, for a first person view — and lockPointer(false) releases it. pointer.locked tells when the source got it: a browser accepts it only after a click or a key, and releases it on Escape. Locked, x and y stay, dx and dy still move. See First person controls.

Gamepads

Section titled “Gamepads”

input.gamepads lists the gamepads connected, in the standard layout of the Gamepad API and of SDL3 — the same buttons at the same places on every gamepad:

Buttons Axes
south, east, west, north (A, B, X, Y on Xbox) leftX, leftY, rightX, rightY
leftShoulder, rightShoulder from -1 to 1, y positive downwards
leftTrigger, rightTrigger, analog
back, start, guide, leftStick, rightStick
dpadUp, dpadDown, dpadLeft, dpadRight
  • value(button) goes from 0 to 1 (analog for the triggers); a button is down beyond 0.5.
  • stick('left') gives { x, y } through a radial dead zone (deadZone, 0.15 by default): a stick at rest is never exactly at 0. The zone is on the length of the vector, so that the diagonals keep their direction, and the value is rescaled beyond it, so that it does not jump.
  • In a browser, a gamepad shows once a button is pressed (a rule of the browsers, against fingerprinting); only those of the standard layout are read.

gamepad.rumble(strong, weak, duration) shakes a gamepad: its strong (low frequency) and weak (high frequency) motors from 0 to 1, for duration seconds. Without motors, or in a browser without vibrationActuator, nothing happens.

input.lastDevice is the device used last — 'keyboard', 'pointer' or 'gamepad': to aim with the mouse or a stick, or to show the right buttons.

Actions

Section titled “Actions”

An application should not read KeyW or leftX here and there. It declares actions, and their bindings:

Type Read Bindings
button button(name): down, pressed, released, value Keys, pointer buttons, gamepad buttons
axis axis(name): a number A stick axis, pointer:wheel, pointer:deltaX, or { negative, positive }
vector vector(name): { x, y }, length 0 to 1, y up or forward { up, down, left, right }, a stick, or pointer:delta

The controls are strings — they save and compare as they are:

Control Meaning
key:<code> A key, by position: key:KeyW, key:ArrowUp
pointer:<button> A pointer button: pointer:0
pointer:wheel The wheel, an axis: the notches of the frame
pointer:delta The movement of the pointer during the frame, a vector
pointer:deltaX, deltaY The same, as axes (y positive downwards)
pointer:locked A button, down while the pointer is locked
gamepad:<button> A gamepad button: gamepad:south, gamepad:rightTrigger
gamepad:<axis> A stick axis, through its dead zone: gamepad:leftX
gamepad:leftStick, rightStick A stick, for a vector (as a button: the stick pressed)
  • Several bindings per action: the strongest wins — the keyboard and a stick do not add up. A button is down when any of its controls is.
  • Four keys of a vector are normalized: the diagonal is not faster.
  • A vector goes up: y is positive forward, while the raw axis of a stick is positive downwards. The binding of a stick turns it.
  • Every gamepad drives the actions by default. For a game with a gamepad per player, new ActionMap(input, definitions, { gamepad: 1 }) reads only the second one. actions.rumble(strong, weak, duration) shakes the gamepads of the actions.

Contexts

Section titled “Contexts”

A menu and a game read the same keys differently: give each its own ActionMap, and enable one at a time. Disabled (enabled = false, or the enabled: false option), a map reads nothing: buttons released, axes and vectors at 0. A map enabled during a frame reads nothing until the next update(): the press that opened a menu does not also act in it. active tells whether the map reads its actions in this frame.

Tests and replays

Section titled “Tests and replays”

FakeInputSource is a source driven by code: keyDown(code), keyUp(code), connectGamepad(), setButton(), setAxis(), and sink for the pointer. It tests the logic of an application without a browser nor a window, and replays recorded inputs.

A source of your own

Section titled “A source of your own”

An InputSource writes the events into the InputSink that Input gives to its attach(), and fills the states of the gamepads in readGamepads(out), reusing the objects of out: nothing is allocated per frame. setPointerLock(locked) and rumble(gamepad, strong, weak, duration) are optional: a source without them never locks the pointer nor shakes a gamepad; with them, it tells the sink when the lock is taken or lost (pointerLock(locked)), and gives the relative movements in pointerMove(x, y, dx, dy). A touch screen, a network replay or another windowing library is one more source.

Camera navigation

Section titled “Camera navigation”

@fdw/engine-camera moves a camera with actions: OrbitOperator turns around a point, FlyOperator flies or walks, with default bindings for the keyboard, the mouse and the gamepads. See the Camera navigation tutorial.

Section titled “Not yet”
