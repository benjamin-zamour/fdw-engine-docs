Ray tracing

The engine rasterizes by default; rays come on top, as an option of the rendering, on the backends that can trace them. The same scene, the same materials: only what draws the pixels changes. The tutorials show each use: shadows by rays, reflections and ambient occlusion, the path tracer, animated and generated geometry.

The backends

Section titled “The backends”
Backend Rays
Vulkan In hardware: VK_KHR_acceleration_structure and VK_KHR_ray_query, where the GPU has them (RTX, RDNA2 and later; lavapipe in software); the ray tracing pipelines (VK_KHR_ray_tracing_pipeline) as an option
Direct3D 12 In hardware: DXR 1.1 (ray queries, Shader Model 6.5), where the GPU has it (RTX, RDNA2 and later); the ray tracing pipelines (state objects, DispatchRays) as an option
WebGPU In software: a BVH built on the CPU, in storage buffers, traversed by WGSL code the backend adds to the programs that trace rays
WebGL2 None: the rasterization only

The capability rayQuery of the backend says whether it traces rays (backend.capabilities?.rayQuery). Without it, RayScene and the passes of rays throw: check it, and keep the rasterization (the examples of the tutorials do).

The traced scene

Section titled “The traced scene”

RayScene (@fdw/engine-raytracing) holds what rays see:

  • A bottom level per mesh, built once (on the GPU in Vulkan and Direct3D 12, on the CPU in WebGPU: a binned SAH), kept while the mesh does not change.

  • The instances, rewritten at each update: every item of the source (all of them, not only those the camera sees: rays look behind), each with its world matrix, its material and its mask (the transparent items and those with castShadow: false are not seen by the shadow rays).

  • The data of the hits, in storage buffers shared by both backends: the instances (their matrices, their material, their object), the triangles and the vertices (position, normal, uv, tangent).

  • The alpha masks of the masked materials, baked once (see below).

What the GPU moves is followed: the vertices of a skinned mesh (Material.deformedVertices, set by the compute skinning) refit its structure at each update; an item whose material makes its geometry in the vertex stage (a terrain of patches) is traced with its rayMesh. Without either, such an item is left out (the sea of the ocean). filter leaves out other items. The tutorial shows both.

Shadows by rays

Section titled “Shadows by rays”

The materials trace, for each light casting shadows, a ray from the point towards the light, instead of reading a shadow map: exact shadows, at any distance, without acne nor texels. With samples, several rays across the light (its shadow.softness: the radius of a spot, the tangent of the angular radius of a sun) give soft shadows. The shadow maps are not drawn.

The alpha of the masked materials

Section titled “The alpha of the masked materials”

The rays go through the cut parts of the masked materials (alphaMode: 'mask'): the leaves, the fences. RayScene bakes the alpha of each masked material over its uv (its hit program over a square, cut at its cutoff, 256 × 256 bits in the geometry of the scene), again when its values change; the traversals test the candidate triangles of the masked instances against it (Vulkan, Direct3D 12: the non opaque instances in the ray query loop, or an any-hit shader; WebGPU: in the WGSL traversal). In your graphs, g.traceRay({ …, alphaTest: true }) (ray.traceAlpha) does the same.

Passes of rays

Section titled “Passes of rays”

Over a G-buffer (the point, the normal, the base color of each pixel: reflectionOutputs(), outputs of the scene passes):

  • RayOcclusion: ambient occlusion, rays in the half sphere of each normal, blurred along the surfaces.
  • RayReflections: the view direction mirrored by the normal, traced; the surfaces hit shaded with their own materials, lit by every light with traced shadows; added to the image by the Fresnel term, faded out with the roughness.

The path tracer

Section titled “The path tracer”

PathTracer draws the whole image by rays: from the camera, then bounce after bounce, gathering the light of the emissive surfaces, of the lights (a shadow ray towards one of them at random) and of the sky. The frames add up while nothing moves; an à-trous filter removes the noise of the first ones.

It works in waves of compute passes: the rays of all the pixels in buffers, traced together; the surfaces they hit evaluated material by material; then a bounce kernel. One dispatch per material per bounce: the cost grows with the number of materials of the scene.

When the camera or the scene moves, each pixel follows the point it saw: the previous frame reprojected (the point and the normal of the first hit kept), its history kept where it saw the same surface, at most history frames (16) while things move; still, the frames add up without end.

Ray tracing pipelines

Section titled “Ray tracing pipelines”

On Vulkan and Direct3D 12, a compute program that traces rays can run as the ray generation shader of a ray tracing pipeline (createComputePipeline({ program, rayTracing: true }), the rayTracingPipeline capability): a thread per ray, TraceRay with a closest-hit shader writing the hit, a miss shader, and the alpha test in an any-hit shader. The same graphs, the same results (a test compares them to the bit); ComputeRunner, PathTracer and RayReflections take the option (rayTracingPipeline: true). Elsewhere, the ray queries. The surfaces of the materials stay dispatches of their hit programs: hit groups per material would bind the resources of every material at once.

In the editor

Section titled “In the editor”

The viewport of the level draws with rays from the menu Edit › Rendering (or the palette): the traced shadows, the reflections, the path tracer — on Vulkan (the desktop editor) and WebGPU (the web editor); WebGL2 keeps the rasterization. The picking and the gizmos stay those of the rasterization.

The surfaces at the points hit

Section titled “The surfaces at the points hit”

A material is written once: the compiler also compiles its surface for the points hit by rays (compile(graph, { target: 'hit' })):

  • its attributes interpolated at the point hit (position, normal, uv, tangent), its object matrices those of the instance hit;
  • its textures read at their first level (no derivatives), its derivatives 0 (a normal that cannot be computed then is replaced by the normal of the vertices);
  • the attributes its vertex stage computes (g.vertexAttribute) are those of the geometry traced;
  • what it gives: the base color and the alpha, the normal, the roughness and the metalness, the emission — the parameters of the BSDF of the pbr model (Lambert and GGX) the passes of rays use. phong gives a roughness of the same lobe; unlit is emission.

A material that has no surface there (raw stages) is drawn gray, with a warning.

Rays in your graphs

Section titled “Rays in your graphs”

g.traceRay({ origin, direction, tMin, tMax, mask, mode }) traces a ray in the scene of the frame (input.engine('scene')), in any stage. Its hit, a vec4<u32>, is read with the functions of stdRays:

Function Gives
hit Whether the ray hit something
hitPosition, hitNormal The point hit, its normal (interpolated), in the world
hitGeometricNormal, hitFrontFacing The normal of the triangle, the face hit
hitUv, hitLocalTangent The uv, the tangent of the vertices
hitObject, hitMaterial, hitData The object (its id), the material, the user vec4
hitBarycentrics, hitVertices, hitWorld The weights of the vertices, their indices, the matrix

mode: 'any' stops at the first hit (shadows, occlusion). The materials drawn by the renderer get the scene with rays; a full screen pass with addFullscreenPass(…, { rays: scene }); a compute graph through the values of its dispatch (scene.resource(name)).

Costs

Section titled “Costs”

1280 × 720 on an RTX 2060 SUPER, without vsync (the TPS: its characters, its terrain and its foliage in the scene of the rays):

Demo Backend Rasterized Traced shadows + reflections Path tracer
FPS Vulkan 1170 fps 576 fps 275 fps 89 fps
FPS Vulkan, ray tracing pipelines 305 fps 90 fps
FPS WebGPU (Dawn) 560 fps 131 fps 68 fps 33 fps
TPS Vulkan 81 fps 39 fps 37 fps 27 fps
TPS Vulkan, ray tracing pipelines 41 fps 34 fps
TPS WebGPU (Dawn) 58 fps 7 fps 5 fps 4 fps

In WebGPU, the software traversal costs more, and more as the scene grows: traced shadows suit small scenes; the path tracer gathers its frames slower. In the forest of the TPS, the top level rebuilt on the CPU each frame weighs too.

Not yet

Section titled “Not yet”
  • A BVH built on the GPU (the top level of large scenes costs on the CPU each frame), BVHs of 4 or 8 children.
  • The variance in the temporal filter (SVGF), ReSTIR, importance sampling of environment maps.
  • The sea of the ocean in the scene of the rays.
