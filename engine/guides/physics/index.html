Physics

The engine does not write its own physics. It defines what it asks of a physics engine, and plugs one behind it:

  • @fdw/engine-physics: the abstract API (worlds, bodies, colliders, shapes, joints, queries, events, character controllers), its binding to the scene (ScenePhysics: the bodies as components, the simulation in the fixed step), the debug view, and a character controller against the volumes of a scene (SceneCharacter). It depends on no physics engine.
  • @fdw/engine-physics-rapier: the API on Rapier, compiled to WebAssembly: the same module in the browser and in Node (the native demos).
  • @fdw/engine-ragdoll: ragdolls on the skeletons of the animation, against the API.

The tutorial Physics puts them together; Navigation walks characters without an engine, Ragdolls knocks them down.

The API

Section titled “The API”
Interface What it is
PhysicsEngine An implementation: name, createWorld({ gravity })
PhysicsWorld The bodies, joints and characters; step(dt), events, contacts(), the queries
PhysicsBody type, the pose, the velocities, impulses and forces, mass, sleeping, its colliders
PhysicsCollider A shape on a body: sensor, friction, restitution, its pose in the world
PhysicsJoint Two bodies joined: fixed, ball, hinge, slider; limits and a motor
PhysicsCharacter A kinematic body moved by move(): slides, climbs steps and slopes, stays on the ground

The units are meters, kilograms and seconds; y is up. Vectors are { x, y, z }, rotations quaternions { x, y, z, w }.

Bodies

Section titled “Bodies”
Member Use
readPose(out, offset) The pose in 7 floats (position, rotation), without allocating: hot loops
position, rotation The pose, as objects
setPose(position, rotation) A teleport: no velocity, no push
moveTo(position, rotation) Kinematic: where the next step takes it, pushing the dynamic bodies
linearVelocity, angularVelocity Read and written
applyImpulse(impulse, point?) A change of momentum (N·s), at a point of the world or at the center of mass
applyForce(force, point?) A force (N) during the next step
applyTorqueImpulse(impulse) A change of angular momentum
mass, sleeping, wakeUp() A body at rest falls asleep: it costs nothing until something wakes it
addCollider(desc), removeCollider The colliders after the creation

Colliders and shapes

Section titled “Colliders and shapes”
Shape Notes
Shapes.sphere(radius)
Shapes.box(hx, hy?, hz?) Half extents; one value: a cube
Shapes.capsule(halfHeight, radius) Along y; 2 × (halfHeight + radius) tall
Shapes.cylinder(halfHeight, radius) Along y
Shapes.convex(points, indices?) The hull of the points; with indices, the points and triangles are the hull
Shapes.mesh(vertices, indices) Hollow triangles: for the static bodies (a level)
Shapes.heightfield(rows, columns, h, sz) rows × columns heights, row after row; rows along z, columns along x

The shapes are plain data: the debug view draws them, another engine translates them. They are not scaled by the nodes.

Two colliders collide when each is in the mask of the other (16 groups; by default, in group 1 and colliding with all). The coefficients of two colliders in contact combine by their rules (average, min, max, multiply).

Joints

Section titled “Joints”

A fixed joint welds the bodies as they are when it is created. Removing a body removes its joints.

A ball joint turns freely, or within cone limits: B leans at most swing from the axis (a cone), and turns around it within twist; both measured from how the bodies are turned when the joint is created (the rest). setCone(cone) changes them, setCone(undefined) frees the joint; setLimits is for the hinges and the sliders. On Rapier, a spherical joint limits each angular axis on its own — the swing within a square of the two axes across the cone, 41 % wider along the diagonals; before each step, the binding fits the square to the circle where the joint is (each axis bounded by what the other leaves of it): a cone, up to what a step moves.

Queries

Section titled “Queries”
Query Result
raycast(origin, direction, maxDistance, filter?) The first hit: collider, body, distance, point, normal
raycastAll(origin, direction, max, visit, filter?) Every hit, in no order; visit returns false to stop
shapeCast(shape, position, rotation, dir, max, f?) Where a moving shape first touches
overlap(shape, position, rotation, visit, filter?) The colliders inside a shape

The directions are normalized. A QueryFilter keeps some colliders: mask (groups), exclude (a body: the one asking), skip (types of bodies), sensors (seen or not, default not), predicate. The queries see the world as of the last step: a body created or moved since is seen after the next one.

Events

Section titled “Events”

After each step, world.events lists the pairs of colliders that started (begin) or stopped (end) touching: those of the sensors (sensor: true: an entry, an exit), and those of the colliders with events: true. world.contacts(visit) gives the points of the contacts touching now, with their normals and depths: for the debug views.

Character controllers

Section titled “Character controllers”

move takes the movement of this step, gravity included, and returns what was done of it; the body gets there at the next step. One move per step.

PhysicsCharacter is a CharacterController: the interface of what moves a character (move, grounded), whatever is behind.

A character without an engine

Section titled “A character without an engine”

The other CharacterController: a vertical capsule against the volumes of the renderers of a scene, without a physics engine. The obstacles are the volume given to setMesh (Aabb, Obb, Sphere, Capsule: their signed distance is exact) or the bounds of the mesh (any other volume stands for its bounds); and a ground function, for a terrain. The move:

  1. In small steps (half the radius), each followed by passes pushing the capsule out of what it overlaps, along the normal of the surface: it slides along the walls. The point of its segment closest to each volume is found by a golden-section search of the signed distance (signedDistance of @fdw/engine-geometry).
  2. The ground: a surface no steeper than maxSlope pushes straight up — no sliding down a slope while standing — and makes it grounded.
  3. The steps: blocked while walking on the ground, it tries the move again stepHeight higher, then down: kept if it lands on walkable ground (checked by a ray under its axis: a steep slope is no step) and went further.
  4. Snapped to the ground: grounded before, not after, not going up: a probe down by snapToGround keeps it on a slope or a step down.

collisions lists the entities touched. It pushes nothing.

The scene and the fixed step

Section titled “The scene and the fixed step”

ScenePhysics makes the bodies components of the entities of a scene:

The body is created at the pose of the node in the world. Who moves whom depends on follow:

follow Default for At each step
body dynamic The node follows the body, drawn between the last two steps
node kinematic The body is moved to the node (moveTo): animate the node
none static Nothing
  • A node following its body is under the root: its local transform is its pose in the world.
  • The component physics.Body holds the poses of the last two steps; the nodes are written between them by alpha (physics.interpolate = false turns it off). A body at rest stops writing its node.
  • physics.teleport(node, position, rotation) moves a body and its node without drawing the way between.
  • bodyOf(node), characterOf(node), entityOf(body or collider), nodeOf(body or collider): from one side to the other — the hits of the queries and the events give colliders.
  • Removing a node removes its body; physics.remove(node) the body alone; physics.destroy() all the bodies of the scene.

The debug view

Section titled “The debug view”

PhysicsDebugView(scene, world, options) draws the world in lines, by a node of the scene: the shapes (colored by the state of their body), the contact points with their normals, the joints, and the rays given to debug.ray(origin, direction, maxDistance, hit) for rayLife seconds. update(dt) once per frame; enabled turns it off. The options choose what is drawn (shapes, contacts, joints) and the colors.

The lines come from drawWorld(world, lines), written against the abstract API, into a DebugLines (positions and colors): a tool can draw them its own way. The view draws them as a mesh of topology: 'line-list' (a field of MeshDescription, on the three backends), unlit, without shadow.

Ragdolls

Section titled “Ragdolls”

@fdw/engine-ragdoll puts dynamic bodies on the bones of a skeleton of @fdw/engine-animation, against the abstract API:

  • A bone is a capsule from its joint to the joint to, or a sphere on its joint; density gives its mass (1000 kg/m³ by default).
  • Each body hangs on the body of its nearest ancestor with one (or on the body of the joint attach: the feet of a rig moved by inverse kinematics, not under their legs): a ball joint at the joint, within a cone (its half-angle around the bone as it pointed at the activation) and a twist, or a hinge around axis (in the space of the joint, x by default) within limits (radians, around the angle at the activation).
  • All the bodies are created turned as the node of the skeleton, the rotation of each joint kept as an offset: a hinge axis is the same in the two bodies it links, as the API asks.
  • activate(pose, matrix, velocity) creates the bodies and joints at the pose (the world matrix of the parent of the root joint), moving at velocity; deactivate removes them. bodyOf(joint) gives a body, to push one limb.
  • readPose(out) writes the local transforms: the joints with a body where it is, the others as at the activation under their parent; relative to the node at the activation.

Rapier

Section titled “Rapier”

createRapierPhysics() loads the WebAssembly module once (it is inlined in the package: no file to serve), in the browser as in Node. The objects of Rapier stay reachable (world.raw, body.raw, joint.raw, character.raw) for what the API does not cover yet.

On one machine, Rapier is deterministic: the same steps give the same world. 1000 boxes falling in a pile cost about 3 ms per step while they fall and collide, all awake, and 1 ms on average over ten seconds, as they come to rest; ScenePhysics adds about 0.6 ms for their nodes (Node 24, one thread).

Another engine

Section titled “Another engine”

An implementation is a PhysicsEngine whose createWorld returns a PhysicsWorld: its bodies, colliders, joints and characters implement the interfaces above. ScenePhysics, the debug view and the code of a game then work unchanged. What to watch for:

  • readPose writes 7 floats and allocates nothing: the binding calls it for every moving body at every step.
  • applyForce lasts one step: an engine keeping its forces resets them after step.
  • events is the list of the last step; contacts is for debugging and may be slow.
  • The shapes are plain data, translated at the creation of a collider.
