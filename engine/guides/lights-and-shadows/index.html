Lights and shadows

Lights

Section titled “Lights”
Light Properties
DirectionalLight direction; castShadow, shadow
PointLight position; constantAttenuation (1), linearAttenuation (0), quadraticAttenuation (0)
SpotLight position, direction, the attenuation, innerCutoff (0) and outerCutoff (17.5), half-angles in degrees; castShadow, shadow

All of them have ambient, diffuse and specular colors, linear, and possibly above 1. A point or spot light is attenuated by 1 / (constant + linear × d + quadratic × d²); a spot fades from its inner to its outer angle.

The lights are given to each frame — render({ lights }), or addScenePasses(graph, { lights }) — or come from the source (the lights attached to the nodes of a scene). Their values go into the light pool, a storage buffer written once per frame and view: there is no limit to their number.

Only what the camera sees

Section titled “Only what the camera sees”

A light costs nothing when it cannot change a pixel of the view:

  • a directional light always lights the view;
  • a point or spot light is used only if its range reaches the frustum of the camera. Its range is where its attenuation falls below 1/256 of its strongest color (lightRange(light)); a light without attenuation reaches everything;
  • each pixel only sums the lights that can reach it: the view is cut into clusters (16 × 9 tiles of the screen, 24 slices of depth), each listing the point and spot lights whose range reaches it. A pixel sums the directional lights and those without range, then the lights of its cluster. See Hundreds of lights; new Renderer(backend, { lightClusters: false }) sums every light for every pixel.

renderer.stats.get('scene') gives, for the last frame, the number of lights used, of shadow maps and of shadow casters drawn.

Not drawing what is in the dark

Section titled “Not drawing what is in the dark”

When every light of a view is bounded (point and spot lights, with an attenuation), what none of them reaches is black. With addScenePasses(graph, { cullUnlit: true }), it is not drawn: an item is kept when its box is within the range of a point light, or within the pyramid around the cone of a spot (lightReaches(light, box)). The test is conservative: an item near the edge of a cone can be drawn, black.

  • Materials without lighting (unlit) are always drawn: give a glowing object an unlit material.
  • The ambient term of a light does not fade with the distance: an item just inside the range gets it, the next one, culled, does not. Give the lights of such a scene no ambient, and a black clear color, so that where the drawing stops does not show.
  • With a directional light, or a light without attenuation, the option does nothing.

How the models use them

Section titled “How the models use them”

The lit shading models sum the lights, per fragment:

  • phong: ambient, diffuse (N·L) and specular ((V·R)^shininess) terms;
  • pbr: Cook-Torrance with GGX, Smith-Schlick and Schlick Fresnel, as in glTF, plus the ambient term;
  • shadowCatcher: no light, only the shadows: its baseColor covers the surface as much as the light of the lights is blocked there (each light weighted by its diffuse light), × alpha. With a blended material, a ground that keeps the shadow of a product over any background.

A custom model reads them with light.current and sums with light.sum: the compiler turns the nodes between them into a loop over the lights. Given the position of the point lit, the loop goes over the lights of its cluster only. See A custom shading model.

The environment

Section titled “The environment”

render({ environment }) lights the scene with its environment too (image-based lighting): a panorama, a cube texture or a sky function, prefiltered once on the GPU. pbr reflects it (the diffuse color × its irradiance, its prefiltered radiance × the Fresnel of the split sum), phong too. See Image-based lighting.

Shadows

Section titled “Shadows”

With castShadow: true, a directional, spot or point light casts shadows. Each frame then:

  1. A shadow pass (scene shadows) draws the casters into an atlas, a depth texture of shadowAtlasSize (2048) pixels, split into equal tiles, one per light (six for a point light). It uses the depth-only variant of the materials: no color, only the position, and the discard of masked materials.
  2. The scene passes read the atlas: the phong and pbr models multiply the direct light of each light by its visibility, from a comparison with its shadow map, filtered over 3 × 3 texels (PCF).

The cameras of the lights:

  • A directional light sees the scene with an orthographic projection, covering what the camera sees (the bounds of the visible items), or shadow.bounds. Its size changes by steps, and its position by whole texels, so the shadows do not shimmer as the camera moves.
  • A spot light sees its cone, as far as what the camera sees, with a perspective projection.
  • A point light sees every direction through six faces, the sides of a cube around it, in six consecutive tiles of the atlas: six perspective cameras, as far as its range and what the camera sees. The shaders pick the face of a point by its dominant axis from the light. Each face is a little wider than 90° (two texels), so that the filter never reads across an edge of the cube: no seam where two faces meet. A face that sees nothing of what the camera sees keeps its tile but draws nothing: a lamp out of view only pays for the faces whose shadows can be seen.

Both reach towards the light as far as the scene goes: a tree out of view whose shadow falls in view casts it; an object whose shadow falls out of view is not drawn into the shadow map. A light only has a shadow map if it is used, and if something is visible.

What casts shadows:

  • items with castShadow: false do not, nor transparent items;
  • addScenePasses(graph, { shadows: false }) turns the shadows of a view off.

Cascades

Section titled “Cascades”

A single map covering everything the camera sees spreads its texels over the whole distance: the shadows near the camera blur. With shadow.cascades, a directional light cuts the view of a perspective camera into slices by depth, each with its own tile of the atlas:

  • count: 1 to 4 slices; distance: how far the shadows reach (the far plane by default), fading out over the last 15 %; lambda: from even slices (0) to slices a constant ratio apart (1).
  • The sphere of each slice depends only on the field of view and its depths: it keeps its size as the camera turns, and its center moves by whole texels. The cascades do not shimmer.
  • The shaders choose the cascade of a point by its depth in the view, dithering between two near their boundary.
  • One light per frame has cascades (the first with them); bounds is then ignored. The casters of each cascade are queried in its frustum, at the levels of detail chosen for the camera.

The values of the cascades are members of the frame block (cascadeMatrix0..3, cascadeRect0..3, cascadeSplits, cascadeOffsets): cascadeSplits and sliceSphere of engine-core compute them.

Shadow acne

Section titled “Shadow acne”

A surface compared with its own depth shadows itself in stripes, the shadow acne. Three offsets fight it:

  • the shadow pass applies a slope-scaled depth bias;
  • shadow.normalBias moves the points along their normal before the comparison, in texels of the shadow map (1 by default);
  • shadow.bias lowers their depth, in normalized depth (0 by default).

Too large an offset detaches the shadows from their casters (peter panning). The defaults suit most scenes.

Fog

Section titled “Fog”

fog in the options of the rendering shows the surfaces through a fog:

The color moves towards that of the fog by 1 − e−density × distance, the density taken at the middle height of the ray and falling by e−heightFalloff × height above height. It is computed per pixel, on the linear color, before the exposure and the tone mapping: the color of the fog is a linear color. The renderer compiles it into the programs of the views with a fog (the fog feature): the views without it cost nothing more. The background is not fogged.

Costs

Section titled “Costs”

Each light with shadows draws the casters once more; a light with cascades, once per cascade; a point light, once per face that sees the view (up to six). The casters sharing a mesh and a material are instanced in the shadow passes too. Those kept on the GPU (a GpuScene, or the static nodes of a new Scene({ gpuScene: true })) are culled there with gpuCulling, against the frustum of each tile and, for the directional lights, against what the camera draws: the CPU no longer looks at them (see the backends guide). The atlas is shared, so more lights make smaller tiles: 4 lights in a 2048 atlas get 1024 × 1024 each; a sun and two lamps with shadows (13 tiles) get 512 × 512. Raise shadowAtlasSize for sharper shadows, at the cost of memory.
