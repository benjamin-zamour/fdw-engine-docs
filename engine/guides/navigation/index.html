Navigation

@fdw/engine-navigation finds where agents can walk, and the paths between two points. Pure TypeScript, on the volumes of @fdw/engine-geometry and the queries of @fdw/engine-scene; it moves nothing itself: a character controller does. The tutorial Navigation puts it together.

Export What
NavMesh Convex polygons linked by portals: findPath, closestPoint, toData
buildNavMesh A navigation mesh of the volumes of a region (and a ground function)
TiledNavMesh The same by tiles, built again alone: obstacles placed while it runs
sceneVolumes The volumes of the renderers of a scene, in the world
NavAgent Follows the paths: the movement of each step toward the next corner
NavCrowd Agents making way for each other: reciprocal avoidance (ORCA)

The tutorial Crowds and obstacles shows the tiles, the obstacles and the crowd.

Building from volumes

Section titled “Building from volumes”

The steps, all on the CPU:

  1. Columns. The region is cut in columns of cellSize. Each volume gives the vertical interval it fills in the columns it covers: its box for an Aabb, two rays (down and up) at the center of the column for the others — an oriented box makes a ramp, a sphere a dome. The ground function fills everything below it.
  2. Floors. The intervals of a column are merged; the top of each is a floor when the next one starts at least agentHeight above it. A column has several floors under a bridge or a floor above.
  3. Links. A floor is linked to the floor of each of its 4 neighbor columns whose height differs by maxClimb at most (or by cellSize × tan(maxSlope), for a slope), with room for the agent on both. A floor steeper than maxSlope across its neighbors is dropped.
  4. Erosion. The distance of each floor to the nearest edge (a floor missing a link) is spread through the links, straight and diagonal; the floors closer than agentRadius are dropped. The agents then keep away from the walls and the drops whatever their path, and the paths need no offset.
  5. Polygons. The floors are gathered in rectangles, along x then z, as long as they stay within heightTolerance of the first (1 cm by default: flat floors); each rectangle is a polygon at their mean height, linked to its neighbors by a portal: the part of their common edge where their floors are linked. On a terrain, a tolerance of a few tens of centimeters makes rectangles of meters instead of a polygon per cell: the paths are laid on the ground from above, their heights within the tolerance.

The cost grows with the number of columns: 0.25 m cells over 100 × 100 m are 160,000 columns. Build once, when the level loads, or offline (toData()); a level that changes builds again.

The tops of the walls are floors too when an agent fits there: islands, not linked to the rest. findPath does not reach them.

Other sources

Section titled “Other sources”

fromTriangles welds the vertices and links the triangles by their shared edges. The triangles are walked as they are: they should keep away from the walls by the radius of the agents. NavMeshData takes convex polygons of any number of vertices, and links (portals) when the polygons do not share their edges.

Paths

Section titled “Paths”
  • Locating. The polygons are binned in x and z; closestPoint looks in rings of bins around the point, for the closest point of a polygon (in three dimensions: under a bridge, the floor nearest in height).
  • A* over the polygons: the path enters each polygon at a point of its portal, the one on the shortest way from the point it entered the previous polygon to the end (a golden-section search along the portal: large polygons do not mislead it as their middles would); the cost is the length of these segments, the heuristic the straight distance to the end.
  • The funnel (“simple stupid funnel algorithm”, Mikko Mononen): the corridor of polygons as a sequence of portals, the path pulled tight through them; its corners are ends of portals: the corners of the walkable area, at agentRadius from the walls.

Tiles and obstacles

Section titled “Tiles and obstacles”
  • Tiles. The columns are cut in tiles of tileCells × tileCells cells, each built by the steps above from the solids of the tile and of a margin of agentRadius / cellSize + 2 cells around it: its floors are eroded as in a mesh built at once (the sides of the margin are not edges). Its rectangles stay within it. A floor of a side linked to a floor across it is noted with the heights of both; when the tiles are gathered, two such notes facing each other make a portal.
  • Obstacles carve: a floor is dropped when an obstacle fills the column from a step below it to the height of the agent above it, before the erosion. An obstacle is not a floor: nothing walks on top of it.
  • Updates. An obstacle added, moved or removed, or invalidate(box), marks the tiles within its bounds widened by the radius of the agents; update builds them again (at most maxTiles), and gathers every tile in a new NavMesh (version counts them). The polygons are numbered anew: a NavAgent given the new mesh (agent.navMesh = …) finds a new path at its next update.

The cost of a tile is that of its columns, margin included: a tile of 64 × 64 cells builds in a few milliseconds.

Agents

Section titled “Agents”

update spends speed × dt along the corners, passing those it reaches, and never goes beyond the last one. The y of the movement is 0: gravity is the controller’s. Given another mesh (agent.navMesh), it finds a new path at its next update.

Crowds

Section titled “Crowds”

Optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA, van den Berg, Guy, Lin and Manocha, 2011), as the RVO2 library does it, on the ground:

  • For each neighbor within neighborDistance (the maxNeighbors closest), the velocities that would run into it within timeHorizon form a cone cut by a disk; the agent takes half of the change that leaves it (all of it facing one that does not avoid): a half-plane of velocities allowed. Two agents already overlapping part within the step.
  • The velocity kept is the closest to the preferred one within maxSpeed and every half-plane: a linear program in two dimensions, the half-planes added one at a time. When none fits them all, the velocity that breaks the worst of them the least.
  • update computes every velocity from the positions and the velocities of the last update, then writes them: the order of the agents does not matter.

The crowd knows no walls: the paths keep away from them, and the character controllers collide. Its cost grows with the agents and their neighbors: tens of agents take a fraction of a millisecond.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • No off-mesh links (a jump, a ladder) yet.
  • The crowd avoids the agents, not the walls: a crowd pressed against a wall relies on the paths and the controllers.
  • The rectangles are not triangulated regions as Recast makes: an irregular level makes more polygons than needed.
