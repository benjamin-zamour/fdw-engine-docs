The pivot model

@fdw/pivot holds 3D scenes and CAD models in memory, independently of any file format and of the rendering: the meeting point of the conversion (a reader to a writer), the rendering (a reader to the engine) and the export (the engine to a writer). Pure TypeScript, without GPU, it runs in the browser, in Node and in a worker. Writing a reader plugs a format in.

A model

Section titled “A model”

A PivotModel is a flat table of resources and a light scene graph. The nodes reference the resources by id; nothing is copied.

Resource What it is
mesh Primitives: attributes, indices, a material, morph targets, faces of a B-rep
material A material graph of the engine, as data, its values and its exposed parameters
material-instance A parent and values for its exposed parameters, without a copy of the graph
texture An image described (size, color space) and its source: bytes, or a URI
skin, animation The joints of a skinned mesh; the channels of a clip, as engine-animation
camera, light As glTF
extension Data that only a namespace understands: the results of a simulation, for example
brep The exact shape of a body: its topology and its geometry (below)

Every node and resource can carry typed properties (a part number, a mass with its unit) and extensions by namespace: the data of a format that the pivot does not interpret, kept as JSON for the writers that can read them again.

Product-manufacturing information

Section titled “Product-manufacturing information”

PMI is a pmi resource referenced from PivotNode.pmi. It preserves the source polylines and text on their annotation plane, with an optional semantic value and links to a B-rep, face or edge. Coordinates use the owning node’s frame, so each occurrence of an instance carries its own placement. A ModelView can list the PMI it shows. The pivot validates the graphics, targets and saved camera views; see Carrying PMI in a pivot model.

The B-rep

Section titled “The B-rep”

A brep resource is the exact shape of a body, modeled on STEP: its topology (solids, their shells, their faces, the loops of the faces, their edges, their vertices) and its geometry: the curve of each edge (a line, a circle, an ellipse, a NURBS) and the surface of each face, kept analytic when it is (a plane, a cylinder, a cone, a sphere, a torus, a surface of extrusion or of revolution), NURBS otherwise: the intent of the design kept. A loop is a chain of coedges, each an edge and its direction there, maybe its curve in the parameters of the face (its pcurve). The tolerances are as read: one for the body, one for a vertex, an edge or a face that has its own.

A node points to its B-rep (brep); its mesh, if any, is a cache derived from it (derivedFrom, at a tolerance), its triangles by face. The validation checks the structure of a B-rep (brepProblems: its ids, its references, its loops chained), never its geometry: that is the geometric kernel’s, which builds the B-reps, transforms them and tessellates them (The geometric kernel).

Ids and source keys

Section titled “Ids and source keys”

The ids are random and unique everywhere (createId, a UUID): two models merge without renumbering (mergeModels). They change at every reading of a file. What stays is the source key, optional: the identity of the element in its file (an FBX id, the path of a part in an assembly). The state of a viewer designates the elements by id, and by source key when the ids changed.

Frames

Section titled “Frames”

A reader declares the units and the axes of what it reads, without converting: a conversion at reading would lose precision on an exact B-rep.

The adapter or the writer converts, and its report says so. mergeModels places each merged model under a node whose transform converts its frame.

Instances and occurrences

Section titled “Instances and occurrences”

A node that appears several times is a prototype, out of the tree, placed by the instance of other nodes: an assembly repeats its parts without copying them. occurrences(model) visits each node where it appears, a prototype once per instance, with its path of ids from a root and its world matrix; findOccurrence(model, path) finds one again. The path tells the occurrences apart: it is what a selection by instance path keeps.

Materials and appearances

Section titled “Materials and appearances”

A material is the material graph of the engine (MaterialGraphData): the PBR of glTF is a graph whose output is the standard surface. exposed chooses among its values the parameters that a configurator or a studio may change; a material instance gives them other values, and follows its parent when it changes. resolveMaterial(model, id) gives the material at the root of the chain, with the values of the chain applied.

CAD colors are an appearance (a color, an opacity, maybe a material) on a part, a body or a face, inherited from the top down, the face winning: resolveAppearance(occurrence, mesh, face).

The bytes of a texture are in the file, or read on demand by the resolver of the application: textureBytes(texture, resolver).

Validation and the report

Section titled “Validation and the report”

validateModel checks the frame, the ids, the tree (one parent a node, no cycle through children and instances), every reference between nodes and resources, the chains of material instances and the values they override, the meshes (positions, indices, faces).

In permissive mode (by default), what is wrong is repaired and said: a dangling reference dropped, a missing material replaced by a neutral one, a cycle broken, an invalid frame replaced by that of glTF. In strict mode, the first loss throws a PivotError (strict) with its line.

A ConversionReport gathers the lines of the reader, of the validation and of the writer: what happened (kept, degraded, lost, defaulted, invalid), its severity, its reason, the element and the field concerned, and the stage that wrote it. A loss is never silent.

Readers, writers and the registry

Section titled “Readers, writers and the registry”

A reader is read(source, options) → Promise<PivotModel>; a writer is write(model, options) → Promise<PivotFile[]>. Both write their losses to the report of the options. Their plugin declares them with the capability manifest: the capabilities pivot.reader and pivot.writer, their formats, their extensions, what they carry, and for a reader whether it streams and reads a resource on demand (a part of an assembly when it becomes visible).

read chooses the reader by the extension of the source (or a format) and validates what it reads; write validates, then writes; convert does both with one report. The code of a plugin is loaded at its first use. A writer of several extensions learns the one asked for (options.extension: .gltf or .glb).

glTF

Section titled “glTF”

registerGltf(registry) of @fdw/engine-gltf plugs in the reader and the writer of glTF, on the code of the loader of the engine: the materials as graphs, the textures beside a .gltf read by the resolver, the extensions it does not interpret kept as they are; what glTF cannot hold (a graph of its own, instances, CAD colors, another frame) written as best it can and said. Converting a file shows it.

STEP

Section titled “STEP”

registerStep(registry) of @fdw/pivot-step plugs in the reader of STEP (.step, .stp, .p21; AP203, AP214, AP242): the exact B-reps of the solids, the shells and the faceted solids, their analytic surfaces kept; the products and their instances, each part a prototype placed by its occurrences; the colors and transparencies of the solids, shells and faces; the units and the tolerance of the file. What it does not read (the PMI, the layers, the colors of curves) is a line of the report, by type of entity. Its B-reps are drawn once tessellated by a geometric kernel. Reading a STEP file shows it.

BREP

Section titled “BREP”

registerBrep(registry) of @fdw/pivot-brep plugs in the reader of the BREP files of OpenCascade, in text (.brep, .brp): the solids, shells and faces as B-reps, a compound as nodes placed by its locations, a shape placed several times one B-rep. BREP has no names, colors nor units (millimeters by default, and said). Reading a BREP file shows it.

FreeCAD

Section titled “FreeCAD”

registerFcstd(registry) of @fdw/pivot-fcstd plugs in the reader of FreeCAD’s documents (.FCStd): the shapes shown as B-reps where FreeCAD places them, the parts, bodies and groups as nodes, the links as instances, the colors of the views; and the construction history as the feature tree of the model (model.features: each object, its type, its parameters, its expressions, the B-reps it produced). Reading a FreeCAD document shows it.

Simulation results

Section titled “Simulation results”

@fdw/pivot-results puts the results of a simulation in the pivot, in the namespace simulation.results: extension resources the core keeps without reading them. A result set has an axis of steps (times, load cases, eigenmodes), its result meshes (the solver’s points and cells, as read, the cell types of VTK) and its fields (a kind, components, a unit declared with the data, a location kept as read: points, cells or integration points); the values of a field at a step are a resource of their own, deferred until a FieldCache reads them, within a budget of bytes. registerVtk(registry) of @fdw/pivot-vtk plugs in the reader of VTK: legacy files, .vtu, .vtp, and their series (.series, .pvd), read on demand. Reading simulation results shows it.

In the engine

Section titled “In the engine”

@fdw/engine-pivot links the pivot and the engine, both ways. installPivot(store, registry) loads the files of the readers of a registry as models of the engine (ModelType, then instantiate): the frame converted under a root node, a node per occurrence with its stable id, the meshes of the instances shared, a graph material per material, a material per CAD color, the textures of the file as sub-resources, those beside it read by the store when they are needed. The engine keeps the link (model.ext#pivot): the structure of the model without its vertices nor its image bytes, and where each node and primitive comes from. exportScene(scene, registry, { store, file }) writes a scene in any format of the registry, its nodes keeping their ids and, for those read from a pivot model, their source keys and properties. The pivot in the engine shows both. Given a geometric kernel (installPivot(store, registry, { kernel })), the loader tessellates the B-reps without meshes.

What comes next

Section titled “What comes next”

Writers of STEP, BREP and FCStd; the loader of glTF of the engine made the reader of the pivot followed by the adapter, one path to the GPU.
