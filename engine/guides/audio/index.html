Audio

The engine does not mix sound itself: it defines what it asks of an audio engine, and plays on the one of the platform.

  • @fdw/engine-audio: the abstract API (engine, buses, clips, streams, voices, listener), the effects (SoundEffect) and the music (MusicPlayer), the binding to the scene (SceneAudio), the loaders of the resource system, and a FakeAudioEngine for the tests. It depends on no audio library.
  • @fdw/engine-audio-web: the API on WebAudio, in a page.
  • @fdw/engine-audio-native: the API on miniaudio, in Node (the native demos), through the addon of @fdw/engine-native.

The tutorial Audio puts them together.

The API

Section titled “The API”
Interface What it is
AudioEngine An implementation: decodes, mixes, plays; its buses, its listener, its voices, update()
AudioBus A mix at a volume: volume, muted, stopAll(fadeOut); the master bus is the output
AudioClip Decoded samples: duration, sampleRate, channels, frames, byteLength, dispose()
AudioStream Encoded bytes, decoded while playing: duration (NaN when unknown), byteLength, dispose()
Voice A source playing: state, time, volume, pitch, pan, loop, fades, stop, ended
AudioListener Where the sound is heard from: setPose(position, forward, up), setMatrix(worldMatrix)

Volumes are linear gains (1 is unchanged, 0.5 about 6 dB lower); times are seconds; positions are meters, y up, as in the scene.

The engine

Section titled “The engine”
Member Use
createBus(name, { parent, volume }) A bus, under the master unless told; bus(name) finds it
createClip(channels, sampleRate) A clip from samples, one Float32Array per channel: a sound made by code
decodeClip(bytes, { signal }) A clip from a WAV, Ogg Vorbis, MP3 or FLAC file
createStream(bytes, { signal }) A stream of such a file, kept encoded
play(source, options) A new voice
voices The voices alive: playing, paused or fading out
state, resume(), suspend() running, suspended (a page before a gesture) or closed
time, sampleRate The clock of the mix
update() At each frame: the voices that ended are freed, their ended resolved
destroy() Stops everything, frees the engine

Playing

Section titled “Playing”

A voice has two gains, multiplied: its volume, set at once, and its fade, moved over time by fadeTo(gain, seconds), fadeIn and stop(fadeOut). A voice stopped or at its end is gone: playing the source again makes a new voice.

Panning: a mono source goes to both ears at full gain; pan moves one side into the other — at -1, everything is in the left ear. A stereo source keeps its sides at 0.

Distance, for a spatial voice at d meters, r its refDistance, f its rolloff, m its maxDistance (the models of WebAudio):

Model Gain
inverse r / (r + f (max(d, r) - r))
linear 1 - f (clamp(d, r, m) - r) / (m - r)
exponential (max(d, r) / r)^-f

There is no Doppler effect.

Effects and music

Section titled “Effects and music”

SoundEffect plays a short sound often: variations (one drawn per play, never the same twice in a row), volumeVariation and pitchVariation (± fraction), maxVoices (the oldest stops for the newest, faded in stealFade seconds), cooldown; play(overrides), playAt(position), voices, stop(fadeOut). A random function makes it deterministic.

MusicPlayer plays one track at a time, looped by default, crossfading from one to the next (crossfade seconds); track, voice, play(track, { crossfade, volume, loop, offset, restart }), stop(fadeOut), pause(), resume().

The scene

Section titled “The scene”

SceneAudio(scene, engine):

  • listener: the node the listener follows (its world matrix, looking along -z);
  • play(node, source, options): a spatial voice at the node, which follows it; attach(node, voice) for a voice played elsewhere; voicesOf(node);
  • update(): the listener and the voices where their nodes are, then engine.update(); install(scheduler) makes it the audio system of the render phase;
  • the voices of a node are a component of its entity (audio emitter): they stop, faded in removeFade seconds, when the node is removed.

Resources

Section titled “Resources”

installAudio(store, engine) registers two loaders and the AudioEngineService:

Type Value Loader
AudioClipType AudioClip decodeClip of the bytes
AudioStreamType AudioStream createStream of the bytes

Both recognize .wav, .ogg, .oga, .mp3, .flac, their MIME types, and the first bytes of the files (sniffAudioFormat). Their size, for the budget of the cache, is their byteLength; a released entry disposes of its sound. encodeWav(channels, sampleRate) writes a WAV file from samples.

WebAudio

Section titled “WebAudio”
  • The clips are AudioBuffers, resampled to the rate of the context by decodeAudioData; the buses are GainNodes; a voice is a source, a gain, a fade gain, and a StereoPannerNode or a PannerNode.
  • The streams are media elements reading the bytes from an object URL (MediaElementAudioSourceNode): the browser decodes them as they play. The duration comes from the metadata of the file.
  • The gesture: a page cannot start its sound by itself. The context starts suspended; resume() from a click or a key starts it, and unlockAudio(engine, target) does it at the first one. The voices played before are kept: they are heard from then.
  • panningModel: equalpower by default; HRTF places the sounds better with headphones, for more work.
  • context: an OfflineAudioContext renders the mix into a buffer, faster than real time (the tests). Without media elements there, the streams are decoded at once.

miniaudio

Section titled “miniaudio”
  • miniaudio (0.11, public domain or MIT-0) is compiled into the addon of @fdw/engine-native, with its decoders (WAV, FLAC, MP3) and stb_vorbis (Ogg Vorbis). It mixes on its own thread, on the default device: PulseAudio or ALSA on Linux, CoreAudio on macOS, WASAPI on Windows — and a null device when there is none, so the code runs the same on a server.
  • The clips are decoded on the thread pool of libuv, never on the JavaScript thread, at the rate of the file; a voice resamples. A clip is shared by its voices, and freed once the last one is done.
  • A stream keeps the bytes of the file in native memory; each voice has its own decoder, run by the mixing thread.
  • offline: true: no device; render(out) mixes the next frames into an array (the tests, or a sound rendered to a file).
  • The library has a C API (native/include/fdw/audio.h), usable by another host.

Differences between the engines

Section titled “Differences between the engines”

They follow the same contract, checked by the same kinds of tests (the voices end, loop, pause, fade; the buses; the distance models; the decoders). What differs:

  • The panning between the ears of a spatial voice: equal power in WebAudio, the spatializer of miniaudio natively (it keeps a little of the sound in the far ear). The distance attenuation is the same.
  • The start: immediate natively; after a gesture in a page.
  • The time of a voice natively runs a few milliseconds ahead: the mixer reads its sources by blocks.

Plugging another engine

Section titled “Plugging another engine”

Implement AudioEngine (FMOD, Wwise, OpenAL, a mixer of yours), with its AudioBus, AudioClip, AudioStream and Voice. resolvePlay(options) gives the defaults of play, AudioListenerPose keeps the pose of the listener and hands it to you. SoundEffect, MusicPlayer, SceneAudio and the loaders are written against the API: they work as they are.

FakeAudioEngine is such an engine, without sound: advance(seconds) moves its clock and its voices, which keep what they were told (positions, fades, volumes). The tests of a game use it.
