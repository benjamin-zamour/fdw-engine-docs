Reflections and ambient occlusion by rays

Reflections in screen space only see what is on screen; ambient occlusion in screen space only guesses from the depths. Rays see the whole scene: the back of the balls in the floor, what is behind the camera, the underside of a table.

Click the canvas: the image with both, the reflections alone, the occlusion alone, the rasterized image.

A G-buffer

Section titled “A G-buffer”

The rays leave from the pixels: the scene passes write, besides the image, the point, the normal and the base color of each one:

reflectionOutputs() packs what the reflections read into three targets (with the color, 32 bytes per pixel: the most WebGPU allows): the point, the normal with the roughness in w, the base color with the metalness in w.

Reflections

Section titled “Reflections”

For each pixel smoother than maxRoughness, the view direction mirrored by the normal is traced. What it hits is shaded with its own material: the compiler compiles each surface a second time, for the points hit by rays (the attributes interpolated there, the textures at their first level); then every light lights it, with a shadow ray each, plus the ambient light. The rays that hit nothing see the sky. The light reflected is added to the image by the Fresnel term of the surface (stronger at grazing angles and on metals), faded out as the roughness grows.

The passes are compute passes: one to cast the rays, one to trace them, one per material of the scene for the surfaces, one to light them; then a full screen pass adds them.

Ambient occlusion

Section titled “Ambient occlusion”

From each pixel, samples rays in the half sphere of its normal, up to radius meters: the share that leaves without hitting anything is how open the point is (1: open, 0: hidden). The rays are traced in a full screen pass — a fragment shader traces rays too, with addFullscreenPass(…, { rays: scene }) — then blurred along the surfaces (the neighbors of close normals and positions).
