The sea and its waves

@fdw/engine-ocean draws an open sea. Its waves come from one of two sources:

  • An ocean spectrum (Tessendorf): thousands of waves of every length and direction, of amplitudes given by an oceanographic spectrum (JONSWAP or Phillips), summed by inverse FFTs on the GPU at each frame;
  • Gerstner waves: a few trains of parallel crests, summed in the vertex shader.

The same Ocean draws both, and gives the height of the water to the CPU (see Floating objects).

The waves of a spectrum

Section titled “The waves of a spectrum”

The options describe the sea, not the waves:

Option Default What it does
model 'jonswap' jonswap: a sea built by the wind over a fetch; phillips: a fully developed sea
windSpeed 8 The speed of the wind, in m/s: the higher, the longer and higher the waves
windDirection 0 Where the wind blows to, in radians from +x toward +z
fetch 100 km JONSWAP: how far the wind has blown over the sea
spread 8 How much the crests keep to the direction of the wind
choppiness 1 The horizontal motion of the water: sharper crests, wider troughs
cascades [233, 37.7, 6.3] The sides of the tiles, in meters
resolution 128 The points along a side of a tile (a power of 2)
period, seed 200 s, 1 The animation loops over the period; the seed gives another sea

On the CPU, once: each wave vector k of a tile gets a random complex amplitude, of variance given by the spectrum. On the GPU, at each frame (update(time)): the amplitudes are turned by e^(iωt), ω = √(g|k|) (deep water waves: the long ones run faster), then eight fields come out of inverse FFTs: the height, the horizontal motion (x and z), and their derivatives. They are packed two by two in complex numbers (a + ib), four complex FFTs instead of eight.

The cascades. One tile repeats every 233 m, which shows from above. Three tiles of sizes that do not divide one another each keep a band of wave lengths: the first one the swell, the last one the ripples. Their sum does not repeat.

On every backend. The FFTs are compute graphs: a spectrum, the 2 × 7 stages of butterflies along the rows then the columns, a last pass for the fields. WebGPU and Vulkan run them as compute shaders; WebGL2 runs the same graphs by rendering to textures (Compute), without a change to the code. The fields stay on the GPU, in two storage buffers read by the material of the sea.

Gerstner waves

Section titled “Gerstner waves”

Each point of the surface turns on a circle: the crests sharpen, the troughs widen. steepness, from 0 (a sine) to 1, sets how much. A few waves, computed in the vertex shader from their constants and their phases (uniforms updated each frame, exact at any time): no compute pass, no FFT, and an exact height on the CPU. Good for a calm sea, a lake, a stylized look; the spectrum looks like the open sea.

The ocean

Section titled “The ocean”

Ocean is a render item: a grid of rings around the camera, each with cells twice as large as the one inside it, then a flat skirt to the horizon (20 km at least). update(cameraPosition, time) moves it under the camera by steps of its coarsest cell: its vertices stay on the same points of the world, and the waves do not swim under them. Where two rings meet, the vertices of the finer one fall on those of the coarser one: no cracks.

The material moves the vertices by the waves, each fading out with the distance before the vertices get too far apart for it (fade, 2 lengths by default). The pixels then read the derivatives of the waves: the normal, and the jacobian of the motion, below 1 where the crests squeeze the water, where the foam forms. The waves too small for a pixel fade out, and make the water rougher by the variance of their slopes: the far sea turns into a soft sheen instead of shimmering.

The light of the water

Section titled “The light of the water”
  • The reflection of the environment (a cube texture: the sky of createSky, or yours), weighted by the Fresnel term of water: 2 % looking down, all of it at grazing angles. The rougher the water, the blurrier the reflection (a higher level of the texture).
  • The sun: a GGX highlight, the glitter of the waves.
  • What is seen through the water: the scene behind it, bent by the waves, absorbed along the depth of water (the red first), and the light scattered by the water itself (scatterColor).
  • The sun through the crests: lighter, greener crests toward the sun.
  • The foam, where the jacobian falls below foamThreshold, and along the shores, broken by a noise.

ocean.setLook({ … }) changes them at any time: scatterColor, absorption, subsurfaceColor, foamColor, roughness, foamThreshold, foamAmount, shoreFoam, refraction, environmentIntensity.

Drawing it

Section titled “Drawing it”

To see what is under the water, the sea is drawn after the rest:

  1. the scene (source, items), with its view depth;
  2. the sky where nothing was drawn, copied for the water;
  3. the sea, tested against the depth of the scene, reading the copy and the depth: the depth of water at each pixel;
  4. the tone mapping, to the surface.

addPasses(graph, options) adds the first three to a render graph of yours and returns the image, in linear colors. Without what is behind (refraction: false on the Ocean), the sea is opaque and draws as any item, with renderer.render.
