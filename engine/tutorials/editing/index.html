Selection, gizmos and commands

The editing tools of @fdw/engine-editing, for any application that lets its users change a scene: an editor, a modeler, a configurator in a web page. Click an object to select it (Shift adds, Ctrl toggles), drag a rectangle to select several; drag the handles of the gizmo; W, E, R move, rotate, scale; X switches the axes between the world and the object, C the pivot; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo; Delete removes. The right button turns the camera, the wheel zooms.

…

The pieces, each usable alone:

Piece What it does
Viewport The camera and the size in pixels: the ray under a pixel, the size of a pixel
Picker The objects under a pixel or in a rectangle, by the texture of identifiers
Selection The selected objects (the last one is active) and the hovered one
Outline A line around objects, over the image
TransformGizmo The handles to move, rotate, scale; tested on the CPU
GizmoRenderer Draws the gizmo over the scene, in passes of its own
History, commands Every change reversible, grouped in transactions; undo, redo
SceneEvents What changed in the scene: created, removed, moved, modified
EditingTools All of them on the pointer and the keyboard

The viewport

Section titled “The viewport”

The tools work in pixels of the texture the scene is drawn into, from its top left corner. A Viewport knows the camera and that size: it gives the ray through a pixel, projects a point back, and tells the size of a pixel at a distance (what keeps the gizmo the same size on screen).

The pointer events give CSS pixels: pointerPixel(event, canvas) scales them by the size of the canvas, and toolPointer(event, canvas) adds the button, the type of pointer and the modifiers.

Picking

Section titled “Picking”

A Picker owns the texture of the identifiers that the scene passes write for each pixel (see Picking): exact to the pixel, whatever the meshes or the instancing. For a scene, Picker.forScene maps the identifiers to its nodes; its last argument maps the node drawn to the one to select, or to undefined when it is not selectable.

The selection

Section titled “The selection”

A Selection keeps the selected objects in the order they were selected: the last one is the active object (the gizmo pivots on its origin, its axes are the local ones). apply(items, mode) replaces, adds, removes or toggles; selectionMode(event) reads the mode from the modifiers (Shift adds, Ctrl or Cmd toggles, Alt removes). hovered is the object under the pointer. Listen with onChange and onHover.

The selection is not in the history: undoing changes the objects, not what is selected, as in most editors.

The outline

Section titled “The outline”

Outline draws a line around objects, over the image: they are drawn into a mask by scene passes without color, then a full screen pass blends the line where the mask has an edge. Their silhouette is seen through what is in front of them. renderablesOf(nodes) gives the items of nodes and of their descendants.

Give a name to each outline of a frame: the example draws a thinner, white one around the hovered object.

The gizmo

Section titled “The gizmo”

A TransformGizmo moves, rotates and scales its targets: nodes of a scene, or bare Transforms. Its handles:

Mode Handles
translate Along an axis (x, y, z), in a plane (yz, zx, xy), in the plane of the screen (view)
rotate Around an axis (its ring), around the direction of the view (the outer ring)
scale Along an axis, in a plane, uniform (uniform, the center)
  • The hover and the grab are tested on the CPU, analytically: the ray under the pointer against the segments of the axes, the squares of the planes, the rings. No read back: the handle under the pointer is known at once.
  • A drag goes begin(viewport, point) (false: no handle there), drag(viewport, point) at each move, then end(), or cancel() to put everything back. The targets follow at each step; at the end, one command goes into the history: the whole drag is one undo.
  • The pivot: 'center', the center of the bounds of the selection, everything turns and scales around it; 'origin', the gizmo at the origin of the active object, each object turning and scaling around its own.
  • The scale is always along the axes of the objects: along an axis of the world, a turned object would be sheared, which its transform cannot hold.
  • Hierarchies: a node selected with one of its ancestors moves once, with it; a child moves in the space of its parent.
  • Axes seen end on, and planes seen edge on, are hidden: they could not be dragged precisely.

GizmoRenderer draws the gizmo over the scene, in scene passes of their own: a depth buffer of their own (the objects never hide the gizmo, its handles hide each other), unlit, without shadows. The hovered and dragged handles are yellow; colors changes them.

The tools together

Section titled “The tools together”

EditingTools puts the viewport, the selection, the picker, the gizmo and the history on the pointer and the keyboard: a press on a handle drags it; elsewhere, a click selects and a drag selects in a rectangle; the pointer hovers. Only the primary button is used: the others are free for the camera.

tools.rectangle is the rectangle being dragged, for the application to draw (the example draws a div over the canvas, in tools.onChange). keyDown knows the usual shortcuts: W, E, R, X, C, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z, Delete, Escape (cancels the drag, else clears the selection).

Touch: a finger (pointerType: 'touch') gets a larger tolerance around the handles (20 pixels instead of 8, tolerance) and does not hover. Set touch-action: none on the canvas.

Commands and history

Section titled “Commands and history”

Every change is a command: do() and undo(), and what they need (the values before and after). The History executes them and keeps them; executing a command after undos drops those undone.

  • Transactions: the commands between begin(label) and commit() make one entry (cancel() undoes them); history.transaction(label, body) commits when body returns, cancels when it throws.
  • Recorded after the fact: history.record(command) keeps a command already done, as the gizmo does at the end of a drag (TransformCommand.capture(targets) reads the transforms before and after).
  • Merged: PropertyCommand sets a value by a getter and a setter; consecutive ones with the same key merge, so the steps of a slider make one entry.
  • Removed nodes are kept: DeleteNodesCommand and an undone CreateNodeCommand move the nodes to a hidden branch out of the tree (SceneTrash): not drawn, not found, but alive, with their entity and their components. Undone, they come back as they were; the history destroys them when it drops the command (dispose).
  • defineCommand(label, apply, revert) makes a command of two functions; limit (100 by default) bounds the history.

The events of the scene

Section titled “The events of the scene”

A panel of an editor (the tree of the scene, the inspector) and a save need to know what changed. scene.observe(observer) of @fdw/engine-scene tells each change as it happens: created, removed, moved (the local transform, the parent or the visibility), modified (a component, the mesh, the name, the layers). SceneEvents gathers them into batches, after the code that made them (or at flush(), with delivery: 'manual'), and counts the nodes of the trash as removed.

An entity created then removed in the same batch is not listed; one removed is not listed as moved or modified. Systems writing the columns of the components directly call scene.notify(entity, 'modified').

What to know

Section titled “What to know”
  • Latency of the hover: the object under the pointer comes from the identifiers of the frame before; the handles of the gizmo, tested on the CPU, have none.
  • Multisampling: the identifiers need scene passes with one sample per pixel (see Picking).
  • Transparent objects write no identifier: they cannot be clicked.
  • Hierarchies with shear: the gizmo reads the rotation of a parent from its world matrix; a parent scaled unevenly, then turned, shears its children, which a local transform cannot hold.
