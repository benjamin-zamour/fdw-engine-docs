The geometric kernel

@fdw/geometry-kernel makes and transforms exact solids: the B-reps of the pivot model, their faces on planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori, not triangles. Its capabilities are plugins, declared by a manifest; it runs in a worker, and only ids cross. Here, a parametric flange: a revolved profile, an extruded arm, bolts and a ring, made in a worker, read as a pivot model, tessellated by the loader and drawn.

The kernel and its hosts

Section titled “The kernel and its hosts”

The plugins run in a host, which keeps the bodies; the page talks to it through a channel. In a worker:

Several workers spread the bodies: each body stays in the worker that made it, and the operations on it go there. In a test or a tool, GeometryKernel.local((host) => registerBasicKernel(host)) runs the same plugins in the page, behind the same asynchronous interface. The tolerance (two points closer are the same) is shared by all the plugins, sent with each operation.

Bodies

Section titled “Bodies”

An operation returns the id of a body, never its B-rep:

  • The primitives: a box from its corner, a cylinder and a cone from the center of their base along their axis (a cone may end in an apex), a sphere, a torus, each in its placement (origin, axis, xAxis).
  • The transformations make a new body, with new ids: a rotation, a translation, a uniform scale. A scale by axis or a mirror is refused (unsupported): a cylinder would no longer be one.

Profiles

Section titled “Profiles”

A profile is a set of closed loops on a plane, already resolved: the outer loop, then its holes, each a chain of lines, arcs and NURBS. The kernel orients them; it does not solve a sketch.

The extrusion pushes the profile along the normal of its plane, or a direction of its own (oblique). The revolution turns it about an axis of its plane, on one side of it, by a full turn or less:

The faces keep the intent of the design: a line parallel to the axis turns into a cylinder, a slanted one into a cone, an arc into a sphere or a torus, a NURBS into a surface of revolution; a line of an extruded profile into a plane, an arc into a cylinder.

The errors

Section titled “The errors”

A failure is a KernelError, typed and explained, the same on both sides of a worker: invalid-input (a negative radius), degenerate (a box of size zero, an extrusion along its plane), invalid-profile (a loop that does not close, crosses itself, a hole outside, a profile crossing its axis), unsupported, missing (no plugin provides the capability), unknown-body. Never a B-rep invalid in silence.

Tessellation

Section titled “Tessellation”

tessellate cuts a body in triangles by two geometric criteria: the largest distance to the exact surface, and the largest angle between two facets. Each edge is cut once for both of its faces: the mesh has no crack. The triangles come by face (the provenance of the pivot), the edges as polylines; the arrays are transferred from the worker, not copied.

The kernel does not know the camera: screenTolerance(distance, fovY, height) gives the tolerance that half a pixel covers at a distance.

In the engine

Section titled “In the engine”

A B-rep is the truth of a body, its mesh a cache derived from it. The example’s reader puts the B-reps in a pivot model, without meshes; given a kernel, the loader of @fdw/engine-pivot tessellates them (tessellateModel) before adapting the model as any other:

Each mesh is derivedFrom its B-rep at its tolerance, its triangles by face with the colors of the faces. Without a kernel, the report says that the B-reps are not drawn.
