Text and lines

Dimensions, notes, markings, the grid of an editor: lines and texts drawn by the engine, not HTML over the canvas, so that they are the same on the four backends, in a native window and in an exported image. Hover the block, the dimension, the axis or the marking: each is selected like an object.

…

Two packages share the work:

  • @fdw/engine-text, without the GPU: the fonts, the layout (a string becomes a list of placed glyphs), and the distance fields of the glyphs;
  • @fdw/engine-draw: the lines and the labels, as items the Renderer draws, from any text system (@fdw/engine-text is the engine’s: see your own text system).

Lines

Section titled “Lines”

The lines of the GPU are one pixel wide, or of a width that changes from a backend to the other. Lines draws each segment as a quad stretched on the screen: its width is in pixels, whatever the distance.

  • The arrowheads are 4 widths long by default (arrowSize, in pixels); the line stops under them.
  • The dashes (dash: [on, off], in pixels) are measured on the screen from the start of each segment.
  • The segments of a Lines are one item, drawn in one call; item(transform) places them (the world by default). Changing the segments rebuilds the mesh on the next mesh or item.

Labels

Section titled “Labels”

A text is laid out once, its glyphs read from a texture of multi-channel signed distance fields (MSDF): sharp at any size and any zoom, corners included, with a small shader written once as a material graph (WGSL, GLSL, HLSL by the compiler).

Two placements:

  • placement: 'screen' (the default): a constant size in pixels, facing the camera, anchored at position, like the gizmos. What notes and dimensions mostly want.
  • placement: 'world': in the XY plane of the item, size in its units; the text follows the geometry (the marking on the top face of the block). Labels in another plane go in another Labels, sharing the text system (and its glyph texture), its item placed by its own transform.

Each pixel measures its footprint in the glyph texture along both axes of the screen, and reads four samples along the longer one: a text seen at a grazing angle keeps its thin strokes.

The layout (align, baseline, maxWidth to break the lines at the spaces, lineHeight, letterSpacing, kerning) is that of layoutText, in @fdw/engine-text: usable alone, without the GPU, to measure a text.

Depth

Section titled “Depth”
  • test (the default): hidden by what is in front, pulled toward the camera by bias (a depth of the clip space, 0 to 1) so that the lines lying on a face stay visible.
  • always: always visible, in front of everything.

Selection

Section titled “Selection”

Lines and labels are picked on the CPU, where what they draw is known (the ends, the widths, the boxes of the texts): a pointer within a few pixels of a line of one pixel finds it.

  • Give an id per segment or per label ({ id: 2 }): the hits carry it.
  • What the scene hides: give depthAt(x, y), the depth of the scene at a pixel, in the units of the view (the 'view-depth' output of the scene passes, read back): the lines and labels behind it are left out, unless always visible.
  • The objects of the scene are found by their identifiers: the example tries the annotations first, then the identifier under the mouse.

By default the edges are smooth: lines and labels are drawn with the transparent items, after the opaque ones, blended. edges: 'sharp' cuts them at the outline: drawn with the opaque items, they are also written in the identifier outputs, like the objects.

Fonts

Section titled “Fonts”

The default fonts are embedded in the code, under the SIL Open Font License: Inter (Latin, Greek, the common symbols), then FDW Symbols, a subset of STIX Two Math: arrows, mathematical operators, technical and geometric symbols, circled letters, the geometric tolerancing ones included (⌀ ⌖ ⌓ ⏥ ∠ ⊥ ∥ Ⓜ). Other fonts are TrueType, OpenType (TrueType or CFF outlines), WOFF or WOFF2 files, loaded like a texture:

  • The fallback: each character is drawn by the first font that has it, the fonts given, then the default ones. A character that none has is drawn as a visible box (the .notdef glyph), and reported: warn receives a missing-glyph warning, with its code point and its index.
  • The atlas (GlyphAtlas) computes each glyph the first time it is needed, in pages of a fixed size (4 of 1024² texels by default, one under the other in one texture), and sends the renderers the region of each new glyph only. The glyphs drawn are retained; when the pages are full, the page whose glyphs are all released, released the longest ago, is emptied. The fields are kept in a cache shared by the atlases, keyed by the hash of the font file and the glyph. Give the atlas a worker generator to compute the fields off the main thread: createTextSystem({ atlas: { generator: workerGenerator(pool) } }), with textWorkerTasks() in the worker script of the application.
  • The license: embeddingRule(font) says whether an export may embed a font, from the flags of its license (fsType); a restricted font is left out, and the reason given.
  • Not yet: the shaping of the complex scripts (Arabic, Indic scripts), ligatures, the variations of the variable fonts (their default instance is drawn), CFF2 outlines.

Your own text system

Section titled “Your own text system”

Like the rendering backends, the text is an interface: the labels draw from any TextSystem (@fdw/engine-draw), and @fdw/engine-text is only the one of the engine. A text system lays strings out (glyph quads, a box, warnings), and fills a glyph texture: its encoding ('msdf', 'sdf' in alpha, or 'coverage' for bitmap fonts), its size, retain and release of the glyphs drawn, get of their rectangles, and subscribe to the regions it writes.
