Saving and loading scenes

A scene built in code, saved as a file of the engine and loaded back: its nodes and their fields, the meshes and materials made in code (written with it), a model by its URI, a light, and a component of the application. At the start, the scene is built, saved, cleared and loaded: what you see is the loaded one. Shuffle moves the objects, Load brings the saved scene back; Download and glTF give the files.

…

The pieces, in @fdw/engine-scene-io:

Piece What it does
captureScene A branch of a scene as SceneData: plain data, without the engine objects
writeFdwScene SceneData as the files of the format of the engine: JSON, and its binary
installSceneIo The loader of these files in a store: store.load(SceneDataType, uri)
instantiateScene The nodes of a SceneData in a scene, its resources loaded by the store
Codecs How each component is written and read: those of the engine, and yours

Saving

Section titled “Saving”

captureScene walks a branch of the scene (all of it by default) and writes each node: its name, its transform, its flags, its layers, and its components through their codecs. A mesh or a material is written by its URI when the store loaded it (store.identify), with the scene otherwise; uri is where the scene will be, the references are relative to it.

The exporters give files, not writes: the application puts them where it wants. Here, a MemorySource mounted under saved; in Node, writeFile for each; in a page, a download. binary: 'embedded' puts the binary in the JSON, in base64: a single file.

The JSON stays readable: the nodes, their components, the materials made in code with their graph (written once, shared). The typed arrays (the vertices, the transforms of the batches, a heightfield) go to the binary.

Loading

Section titled “Loading”

The files of the engine are resources: installSceneIo(store) registers their loader. A scene loads as a model does, with its progress and its cancellation; its meshes and materials are sub-resources (level.scene.json#mesh/1), shared by its instances. instantiateScene makes the nodes, under a node of their own (root), and reads the components, their resources loaded by the store and kept until dispose().

A SceneData in memory (captured, not written) is instantiated the same way; wrap: false puts its nodes straight under the parent (the nodes following a physics body must be under the root of the scene).

The components and their codecs

Section titled “The components and their codecs”

A codec writes the data of a component and reads it back. Those of engine-scene are used by default: the mesh renderers, the lights, the cameras. The packages give the codecs of their components, made for their systems: lodCodec(lods) for the levels of detail, physicsCodec(physics) for the bodies, soundEmitterCodec(audio) for the sounds of the nodes, behaviorCodec(systems) for the behaviors.

A component of numbers needs no codec: defined with persist: true, it is written field by field. Its version is written with it, and its defaults fill the fields left out.

The other components (objects, references) are not written unless they have a codec: the state of the systems is rebuilt, not saved. A component read without its codec is kept on its entity, and written again as it was.

Models and scenes by reference

Section titled “Models and scenes by reference”

An instance of a model (instantiate) is written as its model and its changes: a node moved, hidden, removed, a material replaced, a node added under one of its nodes. They are found by their path from the root of the instance (the names of the nodes, trunk/leaves; #2 for the third child when names repeat).

A scene loaded by the store is a model too: an instance of props/lamp.scene.json placed in a level is written as a reference to it, with its changes. The lamp is made once; the level places it, again and again.

Stable ids

Section titled “Stable ids”

A path breaks when the lamp is reworked: its bulb renamed, moved under another node. Capture the lamp with ids: true: each node gets a stable id (a random UUID, kept on the node through the edits, written as id), and the changes of its instances find their node by it, the path kept as the fallback.

The bulb renamed in the lamp, the level still moves it. Its id lost (the node made again), the path finds it, and a warning says so. When the lamp sits on a desk that is itself placed in the level, a change of the bulb is found by the chain of the ids traversed: the lamp in the desk, then the bulb in the lamp ("ids": ["9a1e…", "c27b…"]). The editor saves with the ids.

Exporting to glTF

Section titled “Exporting to glTF”

writeGltf of @fdw/engine-gltf writes a SceneData as a .glb (or a .gltf and its .bin), for other tools: the meshes, the materials and their images, the lights (KHR_lights_punctual), the cameras, the bodies (KHR_physics_rigid_bodies, KHR_implicit_shapes), the batches (EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing). What glTF has no place for (the graph of a material, the components) goes to the extension of the engine, FDW_scene: the engine reads the file back without losing them. Capture with instances: 'expand': glTF has no references.
