Audio

Three speakers turn around a head, each humming at its own pitch: the hum moves from an ear to the other as a speaker goes by, softer when it is far. The engine defines an abstract audio API (@fdw/engine-audio) and plays it on WebAudio in the browser (@fdw/engine-audio-web), on miniaudio in the native demos (@fdw/engine-audio-native).

Click the canvas to start the sound — the browsers wait for a gesture. A click or Space rings a chime at a speaker, M crossfades the music to the other track, S mutes the effects. Headphones help.

An engine, its buses

Section titled “An engine, its buses”

Everything after the first line is written against the AudioEngine of @fdw/engine-audio: in Node, new NativeAudioEngine() takes its place, and the rest is the same.

A bus mixes the voices and the buses under it at a volume: the master bus is the output, the game adds its own under it — the effects, the music, the dialogs — each with its slider. bus.muted silences it, bus.stopAll(fadeOut) stops what plays in it.

Clips and streams

Section titled “Clips and streams”

A sound is played from one of two sources:

Source What it holds For
AudioClip The samples, decoded once Short sounds played often: effects
AudioStream The encoded bytes, decoded as heard Long sounds played once: music, rain

A minute of stereo music decoded is 20 MB of samples; encoded in Ogg Vorbis, under a megabyte. A clip starts at once and can be played by dozens of voices together; a stream costs a little more while it plays.

They are loaded by the resource system:

installAudio registers the two loaders and the engine that decodes for them. Keep the handles while their sounds are used: the store frees the sound of a handle released — or forgotten. The formats are WAV, Ogg Vorbis, MP3 and FLAC; a clip held by the store is cached, shared and released like any resource. A sound made by code is a clip too: engine.createClip([samples], sampleRate).

Playing a sound

Section titled “Playing a sound”

Each call to play makes a voice. It plays until the end of its source, unless it loops, and is gone then: its state is stopped and ended resolves. Call engine.update() at each frame — SceneAudio does it (below).

Short effects

Section titled “Short effects”

A sound effect is played often, and should not sound the same each time:

It draws one of its variations, never the same twice in a row, varies the pitch and the volume, bounds its voices — a machine gun does not pile a hundred shots — and drops the plays that come too close.

Sounds in the world

Section titled “Sounds in the world”

A spatial voice has a position in the world; it is heard from the listener — from its left or its right, softer as it goes away. SceneAudio binds them to the scene:

  • the listener follows a node — the camera, or here a head that does not move;
  • a voice played at a node follows it: it is a component of its entity, stopped when the node is removed;
  • its system, after everything that moves the nodes, writes the positions, then calls engine.update().

The volume falls with the distance after refDistance (1 m by default), by the model of WebAudio: inverse (by default, the physical one), linear (silent at maxDistance) or exponential, faster with a greater rolloff.

Music

Section titled “Music”

One track at a time, in a loop: playing another one crossfades — the new one rises while the previous one falls, over the same seconds. Playing the track already playing does nothing. stop(fadeOut), pause() and resume() act on the current one.
