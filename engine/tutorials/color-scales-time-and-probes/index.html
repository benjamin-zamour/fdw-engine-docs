Color scales, time and probes

A simulation result and a digital twin show the same things: values on a model, in colors, with a legend; a time to move through; probes that read a value at a place and draw it in time. @fdw/engine-dataviz gives these pieces once, for any application, without knowing where the values come from. Its rule: a value is never changed silently. A missing value has its own color, a stale one is never shown as fresh, the time of a result is always one of its steps.

Below, the stress in a cantilever beam over 21 steps: each cell in the color of its value, the legend by bands, the time bar, three probes and the curves of two. The middle cell breaks at the step 16: from then on, its value is missing, and its curve broken. Click the time bar to go to a step, elsewhere to play or pause.

The data: a time axis and a value provider

Section titled “The data: a time axis and a value provider”

The pieces read two interfaces, which the application implements on its data.

The time axis (TimeAxis) is either:

  • steps: the steps of a result (a transient analysis, load cases, eigenmodes), each with its value and its label. The time is always one of them: nothing is shown between two steps that was not computed;
  • timestamps: the history of a source, in milliseconds since the epoch. live: true and now() when the source follows the present.

The value provider (ValueProvider) gives the value of a quantity at a place and a time. Asynchronous (the values may have to be read from a file or asked for to a server), cancellable by an AbortSignal:

A place (Place) is a point of the world, a node or a cell of a result mesh, or an element of the model (a part, by its reference of the view state). A value (Sample) always comes with its quality (good, uncertain, bad, missing), whether it is stale (older than its validity), and its support: where it was read (node, cell, integration-point, signal; interpolated when it was, said).

syntheticResults() and syntheticSignals() give synthetic data in these forms: the beam above, and a pump read every second for an hour, with a lost connection and an uncertain sensor.

The color scale

Section titled “The color scale”

ColorScale turns a value into a color: a palette, a range, a linear or logarithmic scale, continuous or by bands.

  • The palettes: viridis, cividis, magma, inferno (perceptually uniform: the same step of value reads as the same step of color), cool-warm (diverging, around a middle value), grays, or your own stops. reversed reverses them.
  • The range: automatic (fit to values or extremes: the finite ones, the positive ones in a logarithmic scale) or fixed (range: { min, max }). The example fits it once, to all the steps, so that a color keeps its meaning from one step to the next.
  • Out of the range and missing: their own colors (below, above, missing), never those of the palette. A value out of the range is not drawn as the end of the range.
  • For a shader: normalize(value) gives the position in the range (below 0, above 1, NaN when missing) and image(width) the palette as a row of RGBA texels, the bands as runs of one color.

The colors are linear RGB, as those of the engine’s materials.

The legend

Section titled “The legend”

LegendDrawing draws the scale with the lines and the texts of the engine (Text and lines): the bar with the minimum at the bottom, the ends of the range and round values, the title and the unit, the swatches of the values out of the range and of the missing ones. Drawn by the engine, it is the same in the browser, in native and in an exported image.

It is placed in pixels, from the top left corner of the view, in a ScreenOverlay: an orthographic camera whose units are pixels. The renderer draws it over the image with overlay, after the tone mapping.

The time

Section titled “The time”

TimeCursor holds the time of the view on an axis. It has no timer: the frame loop advances it.

  • On a steps axis, seek(time) goes to the closest step, stepBy(1) to the next one; speed is in steps per second, loop starts again at the end.
  • On a timestamps axis, the time moves within the range of the history; speed is a multiple of real time, stepBy moves by stepSize (a second by default).
  • On a live axis, the cursor starts live and follows now(); any move goes to replay, and goLive() comes back. The mode is always known: a past value is never taken for the current one.

TimeBarDrawing draws the bar: the track, a tick per step or round times, the part read, the cursor and its time, and on a live axis the mode, LIVE or REPLAY, always shown. timeAt(x, y, rect) gives the time under a pixel, for the clicks:

The probes and their curves

Section titled “The probes and their curves”

A probe (Probe) is a quantity read at a place. readProbes reads several at a time; formatSample writes a value with its unit and its quality, never alone:

ProbeDrawing draws them in the scene: a cross on their place, their name, their value, in the color of its quality (gray when stale, amber when uncertain, red when bad, the color of the missing values when there is none).

CurveDrawing draws their values in time, from provider.series: each series as a line broken where a value is missing, the uncertain and stale stretches dashed, the bad values as crosses (seen, but neither joined nor counted in the extremes), a dot on each step of a result, the minimum and the maximum written, the cursor as a vertical line. seriesExtremes(samples) gives the same extremes, with the missing and bad values counted apart.

In the view state

Section titled “In the view state”

The probes, the color scale of each quantity and the time are annotations of the view state, saved with the measures and the sections: data.probe, data.color-scale, data.time. datavizManifest declares them as a plugin of the view-state.annotation capability.

The simulation visualization and the digital twin will implement the axis and the provider on their data: the steps and the fields of a result, the buffer and the history of a source.
