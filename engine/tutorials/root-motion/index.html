Root motion

The character walks straight, then turns left. Nothing in the code says how fast it goes or how sharply it turns: the clips do. Their hips travel forward and turn; the animator takes that movement out of the poses — the character animates in place — and gives it to the code, which moves the character with it. The feet do not slide: the movement is the one the animator drew.

Click the canvas and take over with ←: the character then turns only while it is held.

…

Clips that travel

Section titled “Clips that travel”

The model of the animated character has two clips for this: Walk_RM, whose hips go forward at 1.3 m/s, and TurnLeft_RM, whose hips go forward while turning a twelfth of a turn a cycle. The other clips (Walk, Run…) are in place: their hips stay over the origin, the code chooses the speed. Asset packs often ship both, the root motion ones with a suffix (_RM, _Root).

The animator takes the movement

Section titled “The animator takes the movement”

With rootMotion, each update of the animator:

  • takes out of the poses the movement of the joint along x and z (and its turn around y, with rotation: true): the hips stay where they are at the start of each clip, facing as they face there;
  • adds up what that joint travelled during the update, in animator.rootMotion: x, y, z in the space of the character as it faced at the start of the update, and yaw, radians around y.

The movement goes through the graph as the poses do: a blend weighs the movements of its inputs, a cross-fade of a state machine too, a loop adds a whole cycle each time the clip wraps, a clip that does not loop stops at its end. The layers do not move the character: a wave of the hand on a layer leaves the walk underneath alone.

The vertical movement stays in the poses (the bob of the steps) unless vertical: true: then a jump clip lifts the character itself.

Moving the character

Section titled “Moving the character”

rootMotionToWorld turns the movement by the heading of the character and scales it by the scale of its node. The code keeps the last word: it can add gravity, slow the character on ice, or refuse the movement.

Here, a character controller moves the character against the scene, so the crates stop it: a SceneCharacter, which needs no physics engine (see Navigation). A PhysicsCharacter of Rapier takes the same move.

In place or root motion?

Section titled “In place or root motion?”
In place Root motion
The code chooses the speed; the clip follows The clip chooses the speed; the code follows
Instant, precise controls (a shooter, a platformer) Weighty moves, no foot sliding: starts, stops, turns, attacks that step forward
The clips’ speeds matched by hand (rate) Nothing to match

Both mix well: locomotion in place, and root motion for the attacks and the dodges, in the same graph — only the clips that travel move the character.
