A procedural terrain

A procedural material lives on the GPU, but the game lives on the CPU: the physics needs the heights of the ground, the placement of objects needs to know where the moss grows. @fdw/engine-noise computes the same noises in TypeScript as the material graph: the same integer hashes, the same algorithms, the same constants.

In the example, the heights come from the CPU; the ground is painted by the GPU: grass, dark moss where a fbm is high, rock on the slopes. The CPU computes the same fbm to set boulders on the moss, and only there.

…

The heights, on the CPU

Section titled “The heights, on the CPU”

The terrain of the tessellation takes its heights from 0 to 1 in an array. A ridged sum makes the crests, a fbm the hills:

The options are those of the macros of the graph: noise, octaves, lacunarity, gain, seed, footprint. The noises take arrays of 2, 3 or 4 coordinates (valueNoise, perlinNoise, simplexNoise, worleyNoise, or noise(kind, point)), and the hashes are there too (pcg, pcg3d, hashCell, random). The physics reads the same array: in the third-person game, the heightfield of the forest comes from it.

The ground, on the GPU

Section titled “The ground, on the GPU”

The material reads the world position and computes the moss with the same fbm, at the same scale, with the same seed:

The grass has a fine variation of its own, faded where it is smaller than a pixel (filtered: true, see Procedural materials).

The boulders, on the CPU

Section titled “The boulders, on the CPU”

One candidate per cell of 5 m, moved in its cell by a random of the cell (random: floats in [0, 1) per integer cell, as noise.random2…4 on the GPU), kept where the fbm is above 0.3, where the moss is full:

No texture is read back from the GPU, no table is shared: both sides compute the noise. The boulders fall on the moss whatever the seed, the scale or the number of octaves, as long as both sides use the same.

How close are the two sides

Section titled “How close are the two sides”

The hashes are equal bit for bit: the integers of the CPU (Math.imul, >>>) wrap around as the u32 of the GPU. The floats differ: the CPU computes in doubles, the GPU in floats, and the GPU may fuse a product and a sum. The values agree to about 10⁻⁵; a threshold on a noise is crossed at the same place but for a few micrometers. Only the cell of a Worley noise can jump, where two points are at the same distance.

The rendering tests check it on every backend: a compute graph evaluates every noise, every dimension and every fractal sum at sample points, the CPU evaluates the same, and they are compared — on Dawn, Vulkan and WebGL2.

The rock of the slopes

Section titled “The rock of the slopes”

The rock is the texture baked in Procedural materials, read in triplanar mapping: on a slope, the uv of a terrain stretch, the three planes of the world do not.

Without position, triplanarTexture reads at the world position; the normal is that of the height map, per pixel (terrainBase). The height of the rock, in its alpha, tilts the normal by bump mapping.
