Physics

Shapes fall on a pyramid of boxes, which topples. The engine does not write its own physics: it defines an abstract API (@fdw/engine-physics) and plugs a physics engine behind it — here Rapier, compiled to WebAssembly (@fdw/engine-physics-rapier), the same module in the browser and in the native demos.

Click the canvas: a click pushes what is under the pointer, Space throws a ball there, D shows the debug view (the shapes, the contacts, the rays), R starts again.

An engine, a world

Section titled “An engine, a world”

Everything after this line is written against the API of @fdw/engine-physics: PhysicsEngine, PhysicsWorld, PhysicsBody. Another engine — Jolt, PhysX, one of yours — would only change these two lines. The units are meters, kilograms and seconds; y is up.

Bodies on the nodes

Section titled “Bodies on the nodes”

physics.add(node, desc) creates a body at the pose of the node and makes it a component of its entity. There are three types of bodies:

Type Moved by For
static nothing the ground, the walls; the cheapest
dynamic the simulation: gravity, contacts what falls, rolls, topples
kinematic the code, pushing the dynamic ones a platform, a door, a character

A body has colliders: a shape (sphere, box, capsule, cylinder, convex, mesh, heightfield), an offset, a density, a material (friction and bounce). The shapes have the size they say: the scale of the node is the one of its mesh, not of its shape — hence the Shapes.box(0.5) of a unit cube.

Removing the node removes its body; physics.remove(node) removes the body alone.

The fixed step

Section titled “The fixed step”

install adds two systems:

  • physics, in the fixed phase: the kinematic bodies moved to their nodes, one step of the world, the poses of the moving bodies read;
  • physics:sync, in the update phase: the nodes of the dynamic bodies written between the last two steps, by scheduler.alpha.

The simulation advances at 60 steps per second whatever the screen; the nodes move at every frame. A system that acts on the bodies — a character, a gun — runs in the fixed phase, before: ['physics']; a body at rest stops writing its node.

Without a scheduler: physics.step(dt) at each step, then physics.sync(alpha) at each frame.

Rays

Section titled “Rays”

A hit gives the collider and its body, the distance, the point and the normal. raycastAll visits every hit, shapeCast moves a shape along a direction (what a fast ball would touch), overlap lists the colliders in a shape. A filter keeps the queries to some colliders: mask (collision groups), exclude (the body asking), skip (some types of bodies), sensors, a predicate.

The queries see the world as of the last step: a body created or moved since is seen after the next one.

Contacts and triggers

Section titled “Contacts and triggers”

A sensor pushes nothing: it reports the colliders entering (begin) and leaving (end) it — a trigger volume, a zone of damage. A collider with events: true reports its contacts the same way.

Joints

Section titled “Joints”

fixed welds two bodies, ball lets them turn around a common point (a chain), hinge around an axis (a door, a wheel), slider slide along one (a piston); the last two take limits and a motor. The physics playground of the demos has a chain and a door.

A character

Section titled “A character”

A character controller is a kinematic body moved by the code: it slides along the walls, climbs the steps and the gentle slopes, stays on the ground, and pushes the dynamic bodies. move returns what was done of the movement, whether it is grounded and what it hit.

The debug view

Section titled “The debug view”

It draws in lines the shapes of the colliders, colored by the state of their body (static grey, dynamic orange, asleep blue, kinematic purple, sensors yellow), the contact points and their normals, the joints, and the rays given to it. It is written against the abstract API: any engine gets it. The lines are a mesh of the scene, topology: 'line-list', unlit, on the three backends.
