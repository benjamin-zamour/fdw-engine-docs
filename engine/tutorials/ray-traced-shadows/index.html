Shadows by rays

A shadow map sees the scene from the light, in texels: far away or for thin shapes, its shadows blur, float or leak. A ray is exact: from the point being lit, towards the light; if it hits something on the way, the point is in shadow.

Click the canvas: the shadow maps, then hard shadows by rays, then soft ones. Look at the thin lattice and at the long shadows of the columns.

The traced scene

Section titled “The traced scene”

Rays need the whole scene at once, in acceleration structures: a bounding volume hierarchy per mesh, and one over their instances. RayScene builds and keeps them:

The structure of a mesh is built once, at its first update; the instances are rewritten each time: an item moved moves its shadow at the next frame.

Tracing the shadows

Section titled “Tracing the shadows”

The renderer compiles the materials with shadows by rays: in the loop over the lights of their shading model, light.shadow traces a ray towards each light that casts shadows (castShadow), from the point moved a little along its normal (offset, 2 cm by default). No shadow map is drawn.

Directional, spot and point lights cast shadows, as with the shadow maps. The transparent items and those with castShadow: false are not in the way of the shadow rays.

Soft shadows

Section titled “Soft shadows”

A real light has a size: the sun is half a degree wide, and the edge of a shadow fades over a distance that grows with the distance to what casts it. Give the light its size, and several rays per pixel:

The rays leave on a spiral across the disk of the light, turned from pixel to pixel: with 8 rays, the penumbra is smooth up close and a little grainy in large ones. Each ray costs: the samples multiply the cost of the shadows.

Leaves and holes

Section titled “Leaves and holes”

A masked material (alphaMode: 'mask') cuts its pixels below its alpha cutoff: leaves, fences, the perforated screen of the example. The rays go through the cut parts too: its shadow has the holes.

RayScene bakes, once per masked material (again when its values change), its alpha mask: its surface evaluated over its uv, 256 × 256 bits, kept in the buffers of the scene. The rays of the shadows, of the occlusion and of the path tracer test the triangles of the masked instances against it during the traversal (on Vulkan, the candidate hits of the ray query; in WebGPU, the triangles of the BVH), at the uv of the point: no square shadows of the leaves. What the alpha reads of the uv (its textures) is in the mask; what it reads of the position or of the object is not.

On each backend

Section titled “On each backend”

On Vulkan, the GPU traverses its own structures (VK_KHR_ray_query): traced shadows cost little more than shadow maps. On WebGPU, there is no ray tracing API: the backend builds the BVH on the CPU, and the programs traverse it in WGSL, a stack of nodes per ray — fine for scenes of a few hundred thousand triangles, slower beyond. WebGL2 has neither: keep the shadow maps there, as the example does.
