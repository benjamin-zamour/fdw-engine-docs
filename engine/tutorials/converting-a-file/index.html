Converting a file

The conversion reads a file into the pivot model and writes it in another format, with one report for the reader, the validation and the writer: what is not kept is a line, never a silent loss. glTF is the first format plugged in, by @fdw/engine-gltf: its reader and its writer reuse the code of the loader of the engine. Here, a fern in .glb is converted to a .gltf, its .bin and its images beside it; both files are then loaded through the pivot, and drawn side by side: the original on the left, the converted one on the right.

The plugin of glTF

Section titled “The plugin of glTF”

registerGltf registers in a registry of the pivot a reader and a writer of .gltf and .glb (the plugin fdw.gltf, its capabilities pivot.reader and pivot.writer). Its options are those of the loader: the decoders of EXT_meshopt_compression and KHR_draco_mesh_compression, extensions of your own, the bounds of the memory a file may ask for.

Converting

Section titled “Converting”

convert chooses the reader by the name of the source, and the writer by the name asked for: .glb writes one binary file, .gltf a JSON file and its .bin. embedTextures puts the images in the buffer, or beside the file (by default for a .gltf).

The files beside a .gltf (its buffers, its images) are read by the resolver given to convert: the reader reads the buffers, and leaves the images by their URI, read only when a writer or the engine needs them.

With mode: 'strict', the first line that is not kept fails the conversion with a PivotError, its line in error.entry.

What glTF keeps

Section titled “What glTF keeps”

The reader makes of a glTF file what the pivot holds:

  • The nodes, their TRS or their matrix as written, the default scene; the nodes of EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing become a prototype and an instance per transform. KHR_node_visibility hides a node.
  • The meshes: all the modes (strips and fans made lists), the morph targets, the quantized attributes as they are; a source key per element (nodes/3, meshes/0).
  • The materials, as the material graphs of the engine: the PBR of glTF, KHR_materials_unlit, KHR_materials_emissive_strength, KHR_texture_transform, and the graph materials of FDW_scene, exact.
  • The textures: those of the file in bytes, without a copy; those beside it by their URI, for the resolver.
  • The skins, the animations, the cameras, the lights (KHR_lights_punctual).
  • extras become properties (texts, numbers, booleans, lists of numbers); the extensions it does not interpret are kept as they are in those of their objects, and written back.

The writer says what glTF cannot hold:

In the pivot In glTF The line
A graph that is not the PBR of glTF In FDW_scene, read back exact; approached in PBR for the other tools degraded
A material instance A material of its own degraded
The appearances of CAD Materials, one per color; a primitive split by the colors of its faces degraded
An instance by reference A copy of the nodes of its prototype, the meshes shared degraded
Another frame (millimeters, Z up) A root node that converts it kept
The units and dates of properties extras for the other tools, exact in FDW_pivot none
The faces of a B-rep, the history Nothing lost
An extension of glTF kept as read Written back, its indices not renumbered degraded

A glTF file read and written again loses nothing: its only lines are those of the extensions written back, and of the instances of EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing, written as copies.

One path to the GPU

Section titled “One path to the GPU”

With installPivot, the store loads .gltf and .glb through the pivot: the reader, the validation, then the adapter (The pivot in the engine). The model has the structure of that of the loader of glTF (installGltf): the same nodes, meshes, skins and animations; the link #pivot keeps its structure, and exportScene writes the scene back to glTF.

The example converts with storeResolver(resources), which reads the files of the store, and puts the files written in a MemorySource, from which the store loads the converted fern.
