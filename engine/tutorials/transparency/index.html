Transparency

A transparent surface lets what is behind it show through. The engine has two ways to draw one, chosen by the alphaMode of the render state of the material:

alphaMode For How
'opaque' everything else (the default) the alpha is ignored
'mask' foliage, fences, cut-out shapes each pixel is either drawn or not, by its alpha
'blend' glass, water, smoke the color is mixed with what is behind, by its alpha

The example shows both: three panes of colored glass, a glass ball, a fence with holes, and two crossing panes. The camera turns around them.

…

The alpha of the surface

Section titled “The alpha of the surface”

The surface of a material has an alpha input, from 0 (invisible) to 1 (opaque), 1 by default. It comes from a uniform, a texture, any value of the graph:

The alpha only matters with a render state that uses it.

Blended materials

Section titled “Blended materials”

With alphaMode: 'blend', the color of the surface is mixed with the color already drawn behind it: color × alpha + behind × (1 − alpha).

The result depends on the order of the draws: what is behind must be drawn first. The renderer does it:

  1. the opaque and masked objects first, in any order (the depth buffer sorts them);
  2. then the blended objects, from the farthest to the nearest, by the distance from the camera to their origin, at every frame.

The blended objects do not write the depth (depthWrite: false by default for them): a pane must not hide the pane behind it, drawn after it when the camera turns. They are still tested against the depth of the opaque objects: a wall in front of a glass hides it.

In the example, the three panes on the left are drawn in a new order as the camera turns around them: the colors always mix right.

Double-sided objects

Section titled “Double-sided objects”

A closed transparent object, a glass ball, shows its back faces through its front faces. With doubleSided: true, the renderer draws it twice: its back faces first, then its front faces over them. For a convex shape (a ball, a box), the order is then right.

A single-sided transparent object shows only the faces turned towards the camera: right for a pane seen from one side, wrong for a ball.

Masked materials

Section titled “Masked materials”

A fence, the leaves of a tree, a grid: the holes are fully transparent, the rest fully opaque. With alphaMode: 'mask', the pixels whose alpha is below alphaCutoff (0.5 by default, a value of the material) are discarded; the others are drawn as opaque.

Masked materials need no sorting: they are drawn with the opaque ones, and write the depth. They cast shadows with their holes: the shadow maps discard the same pixels. They also write the outputs of the scene passes (normals, identifiers: see Picking), which the blended ones do not.

Their edges are sharp, and shimmer when the texture is far: the alpha of the mipmaps averages the holes and the bars. Multisampling smooths the edges of the triangles, not those of the mask.

What sorting cannot do

Section titled “What sorting cannot do”

The renderer sorts objects, by their origin, not pixels. Some cases have no right order:

  • Crossing objects: in the example, the two crossing panes on the right. On one side of the crossing the orange pane is in front, on the other side the purple one: whichever is drawn last covers the other everywhere. Split them in pieces that do not cross.
  • Large objects near small ones: a large water surface and a fish in it; the origin of the water may be closer to the camera than the fish while most of the water is behind it. Split the large object, or move its origin.
  • Concave objects (a glass cup): their faces cover each other in an order that depends on the view. Split them in convex parts.

When the colors only add up (light, fire, glows), the order does not matter: such materials need an additive blend, which the render state does not offer yet.

The exact solutions, order-independent transparency (weighted blended, per-pixel lists), are not in the engine.

Transparency and the other passes

Section titled “Transparency and the other passes”
  • HDR: by default, the renderer draws in linear colors in an rgba16float target, where the colors blend right, then encodes them (see Colors and HDR).
  • Scene outputs: the blended objects only write the color, not the other outputs; a glass is not seen by picking, nor by the ambient occlusion.
  • Deferred rendering: the blended objects are drawn after the lighting pass, in forward (see Deferred rendering).
  • Shadows: the blended objects cast none.
