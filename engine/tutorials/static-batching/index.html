Static batching

A square of ruins, a market and rocks: 1 600 static objects, and no two alike. Every column, stone, crate, barrel and rock has a mesh of its own. Instancing draws in one call the objects that share a mesh and a material (the walls of the maze, the firs of the forest): here it has nothing to share, and each object would be a draw call. The static batching merges the static objects of a region and of a material into one mesh: about 70 calls instead of 1 200.

Click the canvas to switch the batching off and on. Hover an object: its name comes back from the GPU, batched or not. A fallen drum rolls to and fro: its batch is not rebuilt, only its vertices are written again.

…

Batching a scene

Section titled “Batching a scene”

That is all: the scene, as the source of the renderer, gives the batches instead of the objects they hold. StaticBatches groups the renderers of the static nodes by the cell of a grid (cellSize, in units of the scene) and by material, and merges each group into one mesh, in world space. batches.destroy() draws them alone again.

What is merged:

  • the renderers of static nodes, visible;
  • whose mesh has triangles, with its positions, normals and tangents in 32-bit floats, and no skin (mergeKey(mesh) says whether it can be);
  • whose material is opaque or masked: the transparent objects are sorted one by one, from back to front, and stay alone;
  • with the same attributes, layers and shadow (castShadow) as the others of their group;
  • that the filter option keeps: leave out the nodes of a level of detail, which switch levels at every frame, and those an editor is about to move.

A group of a single renderer is not merged (minCount, 2 by default), and a group of more than maxVertices (262 144) vertices is split.

When an object changes

Section titled “When an object changes”

An object that moves in its cell has its vertices rewritten in place: the renderer writes only these bytes to the GPU, about 0.03 ms for an object of the square. A batch is rebuilt when one of its objects leaves its cell, is hidden, removed, given another mesh or material (node.setMesh), other layers, or a mirror more or less (its faces turn over). The changes are applied at the next flush of the scene, before the next frame, like the bounds and the spatial index. A system writing the columns of the renderers directly calls scene.rendererChanged(entity).

Rebuilding a batch is merging its meshes again: about a millisecond for the hundreds of rocks of a cell, 60 ms for the whole square at the start. batches.stats counts the rebuilds and the moves in place (updates). Fine for a door opened from time to time; an object that moves at every frame is not static, and is better drawn alone.

Picking in a batch

Section titled “Picking in a batch”

The renderers of a scene have identifiers: scene.objectId(entity), the entity plus one (0 is no object). The 'object-id' outputs of the scene passes write them for each pixel, as in picking. A batch is one draw, one row of the object table: the identifiers of its objects are stored per vertex, in an objectId attribute, which the scene passes read instead of the table.

The other queries of the scene (raycasts, volumes) do not see the batches: they find the objects themselves, which stay in the spatial index.

What it costs

Section titled “What it costs”
  • The geometry, twice on the GPU. Each batch holds a copy of the meshes of its objects, in world space, plus 4 bytes per vertex for the identifiers: 5.2 MB for the 4.7 MB of the square. On the CPU, a batch gives its copy back once uploaded (stats.cpuBytes falls to 0 after the first frame); it merges its objects again only if its data is read, by another renderer for example. batches.stats gives the bytes, the batches, the rebuilds and their time.
  • Culling by cell. A batch is drawn whole as soon as a part of its cell is in view, and casts all its shadows. Smaller cells cull finer and rebuild less; larger ones draw fewer calls. The occlusion culling sees a batch as one object too.
  • World space in the materials. A batch is placed by the identity: its attributes are already in world space. A material computing the world position from object.world and the position, as most do, sees no difference; one reading the position or the normal as those of the object does.

Measures

Section titled “Measures”

The square of the example, in the native demo (nx run demo-native:start -- decor, and decor-no-batching), without vsync, on the development machine:

Alone Batched
Draw calls (the camera) about 1 200 about 70
Vulkan 169 fps 833 fps
WebGPU (Dawn) 50 fps 305 fps
Renderer.render, CPU only 1.57 ms 0.14 ms

The last line is a benchmark of engine-scene (StaticBatches.bench.ts): 1 000 objects of their own meshes, in 4 materials, on the fake backend. The gain comes from the calls saved, not from the GPU: the triangles are the same.

Instancing or batching?

Section titled “Instancing or batching?”
  • The same mesh many times (trees, walls, crowds): instancing, automatic, and nothing is copied.
  • Static objects, all different (a hand-made set, an imported level, a CAD assembly): the static batching.
  • Moving objects: neither merges them; each moving object is a draw, or an instance of its mesh.
