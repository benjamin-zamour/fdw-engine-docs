Navigation

Four agents cross a small level: walls with doors, steps up to a platform, a ramp down from it. Each finds its path on a navigation mesh built from the volumes of the scene, and walks it with a character controller that collides with the same volumes. There is no physics engine in this page.

Click the ground: everyone goes there. N hides or shows the navigation mesh, in blue.

…

The navigation mesh

Section titled “The navigation mesh”

sceneVolumes gives the volumes of the renderers of a scene, in the world: the volume given to setMesh when there is one (the ramp here is an oriented box), the bounds of the mesh otherwise. A filter keeps some nodes only: the level, not the characters.

buildNavMesh cuts the region in columns of cellSize. In each column, it finds the solids by rays, and keeps their tops as floors when an agent fits above (agentHeight): the floor under a bridge and the bridge are two floors of the same column. Two floors of neighbor columns are linked when the step between them is no higher than maxClimb (or the slope no steeper than maxSlope). The floors closer to an edge — a wall, a drop — than agentRadius are dropped: the agents keep away from the walls by their radius, whatever the path. The flat floors are then gathered in rectangles: the polygons of the mesh, a few dozen for this level.

A terrain without a volume gives its height by a function: ground: (x, z) => height(x, z).

Other ways to make one: NavMesh.fromTriangles(positions, indices) keeps the walkable triangles of a mesh modeled for it (away from the walls by the radius of the agents), and new NavMesh(data) loads one saved with navMesh.toData().

Paths

Section titled “Paths”

A path is found in two steps: A* over the polygons, through the portals between them, gives the corridor of polygons; then the funnel algorithm pulls the path tight through the portals of the corridor: the corners of the shortest path, at the corners of the walls. The start and the end are first brought onto the mesh (closestPoint), within maxDistance.

When the end cannot be reached — the top of a wall, an island — findPath returns undefined; with { partial: true }, a path to the reachable point closest to it.

Agents

Section titled “Agents”

A NavAgent moves nothing: from the position given at each step, it gives the movement toward the next corner of its path, at its speed, and passes the corners as it reaches them. Pushed too far from its path (maxDeviation), it finds a new one. idle tells when it has arrived.

A character against the scene

Section titled “A character against the scene”

A SceneCharacter is a character controller without a physics engine: a vertical capsule against the volumes of the renderers of the scene (the boxes, oriented boxes, spheres and capsules given to setMesh, the bounds of the meshes otherwise). It moves in small steps, and is pushed out of what it overlaps after each: it slides along the walls, stands on the floors and the slopes up to maxSlope without sliding down, climbs the steps up to stepHeight, stays on the ground down a slope or a step (snapToGround), and bumps its head on the ceilings. ignore leaves out the renderers it walks through: its own meshes, the other characters.

It has the same move as the PhysicsCharacter of a physics engine: a CharacterController. The code of a game written against it runs on both.

SceneCharacter PhysicsCharacter (Rapier)
No engine, nothing to load A physics world, its WebAssembly
The volumes of the scene: boxes, spheres… Any collider: meshes, heightfields, convex hulls
Pushes nothing Pushes the dynamic bodies
A maze, a level of boxes, a prototype A world full of physics
