Crowds and obstacles

Two groups cross a square, back and forth, through each other. A cart goes to and fro across the middle: the navigation mesh is carved where it stands, a few tiles built again at each move, and the walkers find their way around it. They make way for each other, and for the cart, as a crowd: each takes half of the effort to avoid the other, and they pass by a side instead of pushing.

Click the ground to drop a crate, or click a crate to take it away. N hides or shows the navigation mesh, in blue.

…

This page follows Navigation: the mesh, its paths and its agents.

A mesh in tiles

Section titled “A mesh in tiles”

A TiledNavMesh takes the options of buildNavMesh, and builds the region by tiles of tileCells cells. Each tile reads the solids of a margin around it too, as wide as the radius of the agents: the floors at its sides are kept away from a wall of the next tile as they would be in a mesh built at once. The polygons of each tile are linked to those of its neighbors across their sides. A tile can then be built again alone.

Obstacles

Section titled “Obstacles”

An obstacle is a volume — a box, a capsule, any Volume — that carves the mesh: no floor where it stands, and the floors around it eroded by the radius of the agents, as near a wall. It is not a floor itself: nothing walks on a crate. Adding, moving or removing one marks the tiles it touches; update() builds them again, makes a new NavMesh, and tells whether it changed. update(maxTiles) builds a few tiles at a call, the rest at the next: a large change spread over frames.

Given the new mesh, a NavAgent finds a new path at its next update. Here the cart moves its obstacle ten times a second: about four tiles of 4 m each time, under 2 ms with the new mesh.

When the solids themselves change (a door opens, a bridge falls), invalidate(box) builds the tiles of a box again from volumes.

A crowd

Section titled “A crowd”

A NavCrowd gives each agent a velocity close to the one it would like (along its path), that runs into none of its neighbors for timeHorizon seconds: optimal reciprocal collision avoidance (ORCA, van den Berg et al.), the algorithm of the RVO2 library. Each neighbor forbids a half-plane of velocities; the velocity kept is the one closest to the preferred in all of them, found by a small linear program — and when they are too many to satisfy, the one that breaks the worst of them the least.

Each agent assumes the other does half of the work: they pass each other by a side, without the jostle of pushes. An agent with avoids: false goes its way, and the others take the whole effort: the cart here, a striker standing its ground, a statue.

The crowd knows no walls: the paths keep away from them, and a character controller collides. This page has none — its walkers are kept on the mesh by closestPoint after each move.

In a game

Section titled “In a game”

The third-person game walks its orcs on the navigation mesh of its forest (the trunks as volumes, the ground of the terrain within heightTolerance), and moves them as a crowd: those striking hold their ground, the others go around them.
